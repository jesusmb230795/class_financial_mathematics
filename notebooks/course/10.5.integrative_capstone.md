# Integrative Capstone

Module: Advanced Pathways and Capstone

## Lesson summary

The capstone is a complete investment decision supported by real, auditable
data and a reproducible analysis. It integrates evidence collection, quality
control, valuation, risk, portfolio or resource implications, implementation,
monitoring, and executive communication. A dashboard, valuation memo, risk
memo, committee memorandum, and presentation must all reconcile to the same
frozen input snapshot and decision date.

The final question is:

> Given the mandate or client objective, the evidence available as of a stated
> date, and explicit constraints, what action should be taken, under what
> conditions, and how will the result be monitored?

## Learning objectives

By the end of the capstone, readers should be able to:

- define a bounded, decision-ready investment question;
- acquire and preserve lawful real-data evidence with source, vintage, unit,
  and quality controls;
- reproduce transformations, calculations, figures, and tables from a clean
  environment;
- construct a valuation or resource model with base, upside, downside, and
  thesis-break scenarios;
- report positive-loss risk measures, liquidity, concentration, and named
  stresses under the book's conventions;
- connect the analysis to portfolio, deal, or family implementation;
- build a focused dashboard that exposes material changes and exceptions;
- write technical and executive memoranda that reconcile to the same evidence;
- defend a recommendation and revise it when a stated failure condition occurs.

## Choose one pathway

The pathway changes the specialized evidence, not the common quality standard.

### Pathway A: Portfolio Management

Required decision:

> Approve, reject, or modify a benchmark, active allocation, strategy, hedge,
> or implementation plan for an institutional mandate.

Minimum specialized evidence:

- mandate, benchmark methodology, target and actual positions;
- expected-return or active-thesis evidence;
- covariance, factor, duration, key-rate, spread, credit, currency, or
  liability inputs relevant to the decision;
- transaction-cost and capacity assumptions;
- net performance, attribution, active risk, liquidity, and governance limits;
- implementation orders or a simulated transition based on observed market
  inputs.

### Pathway B: Private Markets

Required decision:

> Approve, reject, defer, or restructure a deal or vehicle commitment.

Minimum specialized evidence:

- public or authorized company, transaction, market, and operating evidence;
- normalized financials or project cash flows;
- valuation triangulation and cap-table or claim bridge;
- financing, covenants, governance, agreement, and diligence evidence index;
- value-creation milestones and follow-on capital;
- exit, capital-call, distribution, fee, carry, and net-investor scenarios.

When confidential data cannot be included, use a public transaction or
redacted authorized case. A synthetic replacement may demonstrate method, but
the final evidence backbone still contains real public or authorized observed
inputs and labels every synthetic field.

### Pathway C: Private Wealth

Required decision:

> Approve, reject, defer, or sequence a family allocation, liquidity,
> concentration, philanthropy, or transition plan.

Minimum specialized evidence:

- authorized client facts or a public/redacted case;
- financial and human-capital balance sheet;
- goals, liabilities, spending, taxes or commitments when reviewed, and
  decision authority;
- stressed liquidity ladder and concentration look-through;
- alternative plans with implementation, specialist review, governance, and
  monitoring;
- privacy controls and a clear boundary between investment analysis and legal,
  tax, estate, or insurance advice.

## Capstone scope statement

Before collecting data, write:

| Field | Required answer |
| --- | --- |
| Decision | approve, reject, defer, resize, hedge, transition, or monitor what? |
| Decision maker | individual, committee, board, GP, family, or delegated manager |
| Subject | portfolio, issuer, deal, vehicle, family plan, or strategy |
| As-of date | latest evidence the decision may use |
| Horizon | investment, forecast, liquidation, and review horizons |
| Currency and unit | primary reporting convention |
| Benchmark | investable counterfactual, policy target, or no-action case |
| Constraints | legal, policy, liquidity, leverage, concentration, tax, privacy |
| Deliverable date | when the decision must be made |
| Thesis-break | observable fact that would reverse the recommendation |

A topic such as "analyze Mexican real estate" is too broad. "Should the
committee allocate 5% of the MXN portfolio to a named FIBRA sleeve as of a
specified date while preserving a \(3.0\times\) stressed liquidity ratio?" is
decision-ready.

## Real-data and evidence contract

### Source inventory

Every input row records:

| Field | Example content |
| --- | --- |
| `source_id` | stable internal identifier |
| Provider and item | Banxico series, exchange instrument, filing, agreement, authorized client schedule |
| Raw field | original label before transformation |
| Unit and currency | MXN millions, USD per share, decimal annual rate |
| Frequency and calendar | daily business-day, monthly release, annual fiscal |
| Inclusive sample | first and last observation actually analyzed |
| Vintage | as-of date, retrieval timestamp, amendment or revision |
| Access and rights | public URL, authorized private source, redistribution restriction |
| Snapshot | repository path or controlled evidence reference |
| Quality status | accepted, flagged, estimated, missing, or rejected |
| Owner | person responsible for verification and refresh |

Data should be structured so another analyst can identify observations,
variables, transformations, and provenance {cite}`wickham2014tidy,wilkinson2016fair`.

### Evidence states

Each material number is labeled:

- `observed`: directly present in the cited source;
- `reclassified`: presentation changed without changing total economics;
- `normalized`: adjusted under a documented analytical rule;
- `calculated`: formula applied to other recorded inputs;
- `forecast`: expected future value;
- `scenario`: conditional or stress assumption;
- `unverified`: required evidence not yet obtained.

An unverified value cannot silently become a base-case input. The decision memo
states whether it blocks approval.

### Claim trace

Assign each material claim a stable identifier:

| Claim ID | Claim | Input IDs | Method or formula | Output artifact | Reviewer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `CLM-001` | example decision claim | `SRC-001`, `ASM-003` | documented calculation | valuation memo, table 2 | named reviewer |

The same claim ID appears in the model, memo, dashboard, and presentation.
Values do not need to be copied manually across artifacts.

## Reproducible project bundle

The submission uses repository-native structure. A compact bundle can contain:

```text
capstone/
├── README.md
├── config/
│   └── analysis.yml
├── data/
│   ├── snapshots/
│   └── metadata.json
├── src/
├── notebooks/
├── outputs/
│   ├── tables/
│   └── figures/
├── memos/
└── presentation/
```

The bundle must:

- declare the Python environment and pinned dependencies;
- run in snapshot mode without credentials or network access;
- provide one documented command that regenerates all graded outputs;
- set random seeds for simulations and record model parameters;
- fail clearly when a required input, unit, or schema is missing;
- avoid absolute local paths and manual post-processing;
- keep reusable calculations in testable functions;
- exclude secrets, raw restricted data, and personally identifiable
  information;
- record the code revision and data snapshot used in the submitted artifacts.

An optional live-refresh command is separate from the deterministic snapshot
build. Live output cannot overwrite the submitted vintage silently.

## Analytical requirements

### Data quality and exploratory evidence

The appendix reports:

- schema, types, missingness, duplicates, and identifier uniqueness;
- inclusive sample dates and observation counts;
- stale, revised, restated, or estimated values;
- outlier review without automatic deletion;
- unit, currency, calendar, and frequency reconciliation;
- any selection, survivorship, look-ahead, or availability bias.

At least one failed quality check and its resolution must be documented. A
project that reports only successful checks does not demonstrate exception
handling.

### Valuation or resource-model memo

The valuation memo is normally two to four pages plus appendices and contains:

1. subject, claim, purpose, date, currency, and unit;
2. controlling historical evidence and normalization bridge;
3. method and formulas;
4. base, upside, downside, and thesis-break cases;
5. sensitivity to at least two material assumptions;
6. reconciliation to an observable benchmark or counterfactual;
7. limitations and evidence still required;
8. a range and decision implication, not unsupported precision.

Use cash-flow, comparable, NAV, or other methods consistent with the claim
{cite}`damodaran2012investment`. A wealth pathway may use a resource and
liquidity model rather than assign a market value to non-transferable human
capital.

### Risk memo

The risk memo is normally two to four pages and contains:

- risk horizon, currency, confidence level, and loss convention;
- market, credit, liquidity, operational, legal, governance, model, and
  concentration risks relevant to the pathway;
- historical or modeled volatility and drawdown when appropriate;
- non-negative VaR and Expected Shortfall when statistically appropriate;
- at least three named stress scenarios with mechanisms, not only percentage
  shocks;
- liquidation, collateral, funding, capital-call, or family cash-flow stress;
- limits, current utilization, breaches, owner, and escalation;
- model validation, backtesting, or reason the measure cannot be validated.

VaR is not maximum loss, and a model unsupported by the data should be omitted
or clearly demoted rather than given false precision
{cite}`jorion2007var,mcneil2015quantitative`.

### Portfolio, structure, or planning decision

The pathway-specific implementation section states:

| Pathway | Required implementation evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Portfolio | target and active weights, funding, orders, costs, limits, attribution plan |
| Private Markets | security, cap table, agreements, financing, milestones, calls, distributions, exit |
| Private Wealth | accounts/entities, liquidity ladder, sequencing, authority, specialist review, family communication |

Each action has owner, date, dependency, cost, success measure, and reversal
condition.

## Dashboard contract

The dashboard answers the capstone decision and contains no more panels than
the decision requires. At minimum it displays:

1. current position or resource state versus target;
2. valuation or economic range and main sensitivity;
3. risk, liquidity, concentration, and limit status;
4. monitoring indicators, data age, exceptions, and next action.

Every chart or table includes currency, unit, sample or valuation date,
source note, and observed-versus-modeled status. The dashboard must render from
the frozen snapshot, expose stale or missing data, and remain readable without
interactive controls. Visual hierarchy serves the decision
{cite}`few2006dashboard,knaflic2015storytelling`.

## Deliverables

| Artifact | Required content | Maximum main-body length |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Decision brief | question, decision maker, scope, benchmark, thesis-break | 1 page |
| Data package | inventory, dictionary, snapshots, quality report | no fixed limit |
| Reproducible analysis | source, tests, configuration, generated outputs | no fixed limit |
| Valuation/resource memo | method, range, sensitivities, implication | 4 pages |
| Risk memo | measures, stresses, liquidity, limits, escalation | 4 pages |
| Dashboard | decision, value, risk, monitoring, source status | 1 screen or printable page |
| Committee memo | DECIDE response, alternatives, conditions, action | 2 pages |
| Executive presentation | decision through monitoring | 8–10 slides |
| Evidence appendix | claims, sources, formulas, unresolved items | no fixed limit |

Page limits exclude source lists and technical appendices. Concision is graded
only after completeness and reconciliation.

## Stage gates

### Gate 1: question and rights

- decision, user, as-of date, horizon, and benchmark approved;
- data access and redistribution rights classified;
- privacy and conflict risks identified.

### Gate 2: frozen evidence

- source inventory and data dictionary complete;
- real-data snapshot preserved;
- observed, normalized, forecast, and scenario fields separated;
- quality exceptions assigned.

### Gate 3: analytical review

- valuation/resource model reconciles;
- risk measures follow sign, horizon, and unit contracts;
- sensitivities and named stresses run;
- pathway implementation is feasible.

### Gate 4: reproducibility

- independent clean run completes from the documented command;
- generated tables and figures match submitted artifacts;
- secrets and restricted data scan is clean;
- tests cover material formulas and failure states.

### Gate 5: committee readiness

- technical memos, dashboard, committee memo, and deck reconcile;
- recommendation, alternatives, conditions, and thesis-break are explicit;
- unresolved evidence is visible;
- a second reader can locate support for every material claim.

Failure at an earlier gate cannot be offset by presentation quality at a later
gate.

## Evaluation rubric

| Dimension | Points | Full-credit evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Decision and scope | 8 | bounded question, correct stakeholder, horizon, benchmark, constraints |
| Evidence and data quality | 15 | real-data backbone, lawful provenance, vintage, units, quality and exceptions |
| Reproducibility | 12 | clean snapshot execution, pinned environment, deterministic outputs, tests |
| Valuation or resource model | 15 | correct claim, reconciled method, range, sensitivity, limitations |
| Risk analysis | 15 | positive-loss convention, liquidity, concentration, named stresses, limits |
| Pathway implementation | 10 | feasible actions, costs, owners, dependencies, monitoring |
| Dashboard | 8 | decision-focused, sourced, readable, stale/error states visible |
| Memos and presentation | 10 | reconciled technical and executive communication using DECIDE |
| Governance and limitations | 7 | conflicts, privacy, unresolved evidence, dissent, thesis-break |
| **Total** | **100** | |

### Non-compensable failures

The capstone cannot pass, regardless of total points, if it:

- lacks a real, auditable data backbone;
- cannot reproduce the graded outputs from the documented snapshot workflow;
- exposes credentials, restricted raw data, or unauthorized personal data;
- omits the valuation/resource memo or risk memo;
- reports return-threshold loss as positive-loss VaR, mixes incompatible units,
  or hides a material sign convention;
- presents simulated or forecast values as observed;
- makes a recommendation that cannot be traced to evidence and calculation.

## Executive presentation and defense

The presentation follows:

1. decision and recommendation;
2. mandate, client, or deal fit;
3. controlling real-data evidence;
4. valuation or resource range;
5. risk, liquidity, and thesis-break;
6. alternatives and trade-offs;
7. implementation, costs, and conditions;
8. monitoring, owner, and next review.

During the defense, the reviewer may change one material assumption, remove one
data source, or introduce one stress. The student must identify which outputs
change, rerun or approximate the impact transparently, and state whether the
recommendation survives. Memorizing the base-case presentation is not
sufficient.

## Submission checklist

- [ ] The decision is specific and uses a stated as-of date.
- [ ] At least one real-data snapshot and its metadata are preserved.
- [ ] Every material value has currency, unit, date, and evidence state.
- [ ] Data rights and confidentiality are handled correctly.
- [ ] One command regenerates all graded outputs in snapshot mode.
- [ ] Valuation/resource and risk memos reconcile to the same inputs.
- [ ] VaR and Expected Shortfall, if used, are non-negative loss magnitudes.
- [ ] Dashboard values reconcile to generated tables.
- [ ] Committee memo includes alternatives, conditions, owner, and trigger.
- [ ] Presentation numbers reconcile to the memos.
- [ ] Limitations and unresolved evidence are visible.
- [ ] Independent review and clean-run evidence are attached.

## Checks and limitations

- Recalculate all material examples independently of the presentation layer.
- Confirm that every memo and dashboard value is generated from the frozen
  snapshot and current model revision.
- Test unit, currency, date, sign, and annualization failures explicitly.
- Treat scenario probabilities, terminal assumptions, and appraisal values as
  model inputs rather than observed facts.
- Do not interpret a successful clean run as proof that the economic model is
  valid.
- Record evidence unavailable before the decision date and explain whether it
  blocks approval.
- Keep confidential and restricted evidence outside the public bundle while
  preserving an auditable controlled reference.
- The common rubric cannot eliminate pathway-specific legal, tax, regulatory,
  fiduciary, or professional review.

## Practice

Prepare a capstone proposal containing:

1. pathway and one-sentence decision;
2. decision maker, as-of date, horizon, currency, and benchmark;
3. three real-data sources with provider, identifier, unit, frequency, and
   rights status;
4. valuation/resource method and two sensitivities;
5. two quantitative risks, two non-quantitative risks, and three named stresses;
6. implementation action, owner, and thesis-break;
7. snapshot build command and independent reviewer.

```{dropdown} Proposal review standard
The proposal passes when the question can be answered with the named evidence,
the sources can lawfully support a reproducible snapshot or controlled
reference, the model matches the owned claim, the risks use stated horizons and
units, and the action has an observable reversal condition. If confidential
evidence is required, the proposal explains how it will be reviewed without
entering the public repository.
```

## Handoff

The final handoff is professional rather than curricular. Deliver the frozen
evidence bundle, reproducible build, valuation/resource memo, risk memo,
dashboard, committee memo, presentation, decision log, and monitoring owners.
The work is complete only when another analyst can reproduce the outputs and
the decision maker can act without guessing which assumptions, conditions, or
risks were omitted.
