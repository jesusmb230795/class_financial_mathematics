# Portfolio Role and Mexican Alternatives Case

Module: Alternative Investments

## Lesson summary

An alternatives allocation is defensible only when its expected portfolio role
survives fees, leverage, illiquidity, valuation uncertainty, and governance
constraints. This lesson converts individual position cards into a liquidity
budget, exposure map, benchmark plan, and investment-committee recommendation.
The worked case uses simulated portfolio amounts and references public Mexican
vehicle types; it does not estimate or recommend a specific security.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- state a measurable portfolio role for an alternative allocation;
- distinguish economic diversification from label diversification;
- construct liquidity, commitment, concentration, and governance budgets;
- compare after-fee performance with an investable or cash-flow-matched
  benchmark;
- evaluate FIBRAs, CKDs, and private vehicles using asset, vehicle, and
  strategy evidence;
- write a conditional investment-committee recommendation with monitoring and
  exit triggers.

## Prerequisites and notation

Complete the four preceding Module 8 lessons. The lesson uses:

- \(A\): total portfolio assets;
- \(L\): assets convertible to cash within the policy horizon after stress
  haircuts;
- \(N\): planned cash need within the horizon;
- \(U\): callable unfunded commitments within the horizon;
- \(B\): required policy liquidity buffer within the same horizon;
- \(q\): minimum policy liquidity-coverage multiple.

All portfolio values are MXN millions. A liquidity coverage ratio is:

```{math}
LCR=\frac{L-U}{N+B}.
```

The policy must define the horizon, stress haircuts, treatment of credit lines,
and whether \(U\) is already included in \(N\) or \(B\). Each expected use of
cash is counted once. Liquidity headroom above the policy floor is:

```{math}
H=(L-U)-q(N+B).
```

## Define the portfolio role

A portfolio role is a testable economic statement:

| Claimed role | Evidence required | Invalid shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income | net distributable cash flow, coverage, stability, currency | stated yield alone |
| Growth | operating value creation, reinvestment, exit pathways | high historical IRR alone |
| Inflation sensitivity | revenue/cost indexation and scenario response | "real asset" label |
| Diversification | factor, cash-flow, and stress behavior after stale-price review | low smoothed correlation |
| Downside mitigation | convexity, hedge mechanics, crisis liquidity | positive average return |
| Illiquidity premium | comparable exposure after fees, leverage, selection, and risk controls | return difference versus cash |

The role determines the benchmark, holding period, risk measure, monitoring
frequency, and funding source.

## Allocation constraints

### Liquidity budget

The budget combines:

- operating cash needs and known distributions;
- collateral and margin calls;
- callable private-fund commitments;
- debt maturities and refinancing stress;
- redemption notice, gates, and settlement;
- stress haircuts and liquidation time;
- minimum policy buffer.

An asset that can be sold eventually is not necessarily liquid within the
decision horizon.

### Exposure and concentration

Look through vehicle labels to underlying:

- issuer, sector, geography, currency, and macro sensitivity;
- leverage, seniority, collateral, and counterparty;
- manager, vintage, exit route, and valuation source;
- property type, tenant, commodity, protocol, or factor;
- public/private overlap.

A listed FIBRA and a private property fund can concentrate the same property,
tenant, rate, and currency exposures even though one trades daily.

### Governance capacity

The allocation must fit the investor's ability to:

- review capital calls, valuations, covenants, and side letters;
- reconcile data across managers;
- monitor conflicts and related parties;
- approve follow-on capital and restructurings;
- execute tax, legal, custody, and operational controls;
- report lagged and estimated values transparently.

Governance capacity is a binding resource, not a qualitative afterthought.

## Performance and benchmarking

After-fee performance uses investor cash flows. Private vehicles report DPI,
RVPI, TVPI, and dated IRR together. Public or liquid strategies report a
time-weighted return when appropriate, but leverage and cash flows must remain
visible.

Benchmark choices include:

- investable public index for a liquid opportunity set;
- property or commodity index with matching methodology and currency;
- public-market equivalent using identical private-fund cash-flow dates;
- absolute-return hurdle that includes the investor's currency and liquidity
  requirement;
- custom policy benchmark built from the approved asset allocation.

The benchmark is declared before performance is interpreted. Sharpe ratios for
smoothed or serially correlated returns need explicit caution because the usual
scaling assumptions may fail {cite}`sharpe1994ratio,lo2002sharpe`.
For illiquid vehicles, the dated investor ledger, gross/net status, residual
NAV, and subscription-facility treatment must remain visible alongside any
summary performance metric {cite}`ilpaPrinciples2019`.

## Mexican vehicle lens

Grupo BMV presents FIBRAs and CKDs as different market instruments
{cite}`bmvFibras,bmvCkds`. The classroom analysis keeps the distinctions
explicit:

| Dimension | FIBRA lens | CKD/private-capital lens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economic exposure | real-estate operating cash flows and asset values | projects, companies, or private assets defined by the vehicle |
| Market evidence | listed certificate price plus issuer disclosures | capital calls, distributions, manager NAV, trust and project reports |
| Valuation | NOI, cap rate, NAV, FFO/AFFO, debt | asset or company model plus waterfall and vehicle economics |
| Liquidity | exchange trading does not guarantee depth | transfer and realization constraints are central |
| Governance | trust, manager, committees, related parties | trust, manager, investment decisions, conflicts, agreement rights |
| Required identifiers | ticker/certificate, reporting period, units | vehicle, series, vintage, commitment, ownership, valuation date |

This table is an analytical starting point. The current prospectus, trust
documents, exchange disclosures, financial statements, and investor reports
control each case. Vehicle labels do not establish tax treatment, investor
eligibility, liquidity, or expected return.

## Worked case: liquidity-constrained allocation

A simulated institutional portfolio has MXN 1,000m. Before a proposed
allocation, it holds MXN 850m of assets that can be converted to cash inside
one year after policy haircuts. The committee considers:

- 7% of total assets in a diversified listed FIBRA sleeve;
- 4% in a private-capital vehicle with MXN 50m of additional callable
  commitments;
- 2% in a collateralized commodity-index mandate.

The allocation is funded with liquid assets. For this deliberately conservative
base case, each proposed sleeve receives zero stressed one-year liquidity credit:
the full MXN 70m, 40m, and 20m is removed from the opening liquid pool. This is
a policy assumption for the worked example, not a claim that a listed FIBRA or
collateralized mandate can never be monetized. More generally, if
\(\lambda_i\in[0,1]\) is the stressed eligible fraction of allocation \(A_i\),
then

```{math}
L_1=L_0-\sum_i(1-\lambda_i)A_i.
```

Here \(\lambda_i=0\) for every proposed sleeve. Therefore, after funding:

```{math}
L=850-(70+40+20)=\text{MXN }720\text{m}.
```

Known one-year cash needs are MXN 80m and the required policy buffer is MXN
60m:

```{math}
N+B=80+60=\text{MXN }140\text{m}.
```

After reserving the callable commitment:

```{math}
LCR=\frac{720-50}{140}=4.79\times.
```

At the $q=3.0\times$ floor, required liquid assets after commitments and policy
headroom are:

```{math}
q(N+B)=3.0\times140=\text{MXN }420\text{m},
\qquad
H=670-420=\text{MXN }250\text{m}.
```

If policy requires \(LCR\geq3.0\times\), the proposal passes this single
liquidity test. That does not prove suitability. The committee still needs:

1. a look-through concentration test for property, rates, MXN, and manager;
2. FIBRA price/NAV and distribution evidence;
3. private-vehicle cash-flow, waterfall, valuation, and commitment pacing;
4. commodity-index contract, roll, collateral, and FX methodology;
5. after-fee return ranges and stress losses;
6. governance owners and escalation triggers.

### Conditional recommendation

A defensible memo could state:

> Approve the target allocation only if the post-allocation stressed LCR
> remains at least \(3.0\times\), no single manager exceeds the policy
> concentration, all vehicle-level fees and commitments are captured, and the
> quarterly report distinguishes traded price, manager NAV, appraisal, and
> modeled value.

The statement is conditional, measurable, and monitorable. It does not claim
that alternatives inherently improve the portfolio.

## Monitoring dashboard

The quarterly alternatives panel should contain:

| Section | Minimum fields |
| --- | --- |
| Allocation | target, actual, unfunded, currency, manager, vintage |
| Cash flows | contributions, distributions, income, fees, carry, forecast calls |
| Value | traded, reported, appraised, or modeled status; valuation age |
| Performance | net return, DPI, RVPI, TVPI, benchmark, realized share |
| Risk | leverage, factor, concentration, positive-loss stress, counterparty |
| Liquidity | notice, gate, side pocket, days to liquidate, stressed LCR |
| Governance | document exceptions, conflicts, covenant events, next action |

Dashboard values preserve source and as-of date. A chart is not a substitute
for the underlying cash-flow ledger or valuation bridge
{cite}`few2006dashboard`.

## Checks and limitations

- Do not assume listed means liquid or private means unpriceable.
- Deduct unfunded commitments once, not twice.
- Stress correlations and funding needs together.
- Compare after-fee, leverage-consistent, currency-consistent performance.
- Record appraisal and manager-report lags.
- Verify current Mexican vehicle terms from controlling documents.
- Separate asset allocation approval from manager and legal approval.
- The worked portfolio is simulated and omits taxes, regulation, liability
  matching, and investor-specific constraints.

## Practice

A simulated MXN 600m portfolio has MXN 420m of one-year stressed liquid assets.
It proposes MXN 45m in listed real estate and MXN 30m in a private vehicle with
MXN 35m of callable commitments. One-year operating cash need is MXN 55m and
the policy stress reserve is MXN 35m.

1. Calculate post-allocation stressed liquid assets.
2. Calculate LCR after reserving the callable commitment.
3. Test the proposal against a \(3.0\times\) minimum.
4. State one additional concentration or governance condition.

```{dropdown} Suggested answer
Post-allocation liquid assets are \(420-45-30=\) MXN 345m. Planned cash need
plus the policy buffer is \(N+B=55+35=\) MXN 90m. After reserving the separate
MXN 35m callable commitment, \(LCR=(345-35)/90=3.44\times\), so the proposal
passes the stated minimum.
Approval could still require a manager cap, property-sector look-through,
current valuation review, documented fee/waterfall terms, or a named owner for
capital-call monitoring.
```
