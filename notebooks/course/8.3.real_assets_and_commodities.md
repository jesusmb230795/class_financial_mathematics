# Real Assets and Commodities

Module: Alternative Investments

## Lesson summary

Real assets connect investment returns to physical use, contractual revenue,
resource production, or commodity prices. The economic exposure depends on
the ownership form. Direct property produces net operating income; a listed
real-estate vehicle adds capital structure and market pricing; infrastructure
depends on contract and demand risk; timberland and farmland combine biological
growth with land and commodity exposure; a commodity future adds collateral
and roll mechanics without owning the physical inventory.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- distinguish direct and listed real-estate cash flows;
- calculate NOI, cap-rate value, NAV, FFO, and AFFO under stated definitions;
- identify the revenue, volume, inflation, operating, financing, political,
  environmental, and exit risks in infrastructure and natural resources;
- distinguish commodity spot return, collateral return, and roll return;
- explain contango, backwardation, futures rolls, swaps, and index-methodology
  effects;
- document a Mexican FIBRA example without confusing a listed unit price with
  appraisal NAV.

## Prerequisites and notation

Complete [Private-Company Valuation and Due Diligence](8.2.private_company_valuation_and_due_diligence.md).
The lesson uses:

- \(NOI\): property revenue less property-level operating expenses before
  financing, income tax, and depreciation;
- \(k\): capitalization rate as a decimal per year;
- \(NAV\): asset value less liabilities attributable to investors;
- \(F_0\): current futures price for the contract being purchased;
- \(S_T\): spot price when that contract converges at expiry.

Amounts are MXN millions and rates are annual decimals unless explicitly
stated otherwise.

## Direct and listed real estate

### Net operating income and cap rates

For a stabilized property:

```{math}
NOI=\text{Rental and property revenue}
-\text{Vacancy and credit loss}
-\text{Property operating expenses}.
```

If one year's stabilized NOI is capitalized at rate \(k\):

```{math}
V_{\text{property}}=\frac{NOI_1}{k}.
```

The numerator and denominator must be consistent. Forward NOI should be paired
with a forward cap-rate convention; current NOI with a current convention.
Capital expenditures, tenant improvements, leasing commissions, taxes, and
financing are not all captured by NOI. A cap-rate value is therefore a
cross-check, not a complete cash-flow model.

### NAV

A simplified listed-vehicle NAV is:

```{math}
NAV=\sum_i V_{\text{property},i}
+\text{Other assets}
-\text{Debt}
-\text{Other liabilities}.
```

NAV per unit divides by fully diluted units. A listed price can trade above or
below estimated NAV because the market also prices management, governance,
financing, taxes, liquidity, growth, and the uncertainty in property values.

### FFO and AFFO

Funds from operations (FFO) and adjusted funds from operations (AFFO) are
non-GAAP analytical measures whose exact issuer or industry definition must be
preserved. A common analytical bridge begins:

```{math}
FFO\approx\text{Net income}
+\text{Real-estate depreciation and amortization}
-\text{Gains on property sales}.
```

AFFO then adjusts FFO for recurring capital expenditures, straight-line rent,
leasing costs, and other recurring non-cash or maintenance items under the
declared definition. Never compare two AFFO values until the adjustment
schedules are reconciled. The Nareit FFO definition and implementation
guidance provide a reference point for the base measure, while issuer-specific
adjustments still require an explicit bridge {cite}`nareitFfo2002`.

## Worked example: property value and vehicle NAV

A simulated stabilized property expects next-year NOI of MXN 12m. Comparable
evidence supports an 8.5% annual cap rate:

```{math}
V_{\text{property}}=\frac{12}{0.085}
=\text{MXN }141.18\text{m}.
```

Assume the vehicle also holds MXN 4m of other assets, MXN 60m of debt, MXN 3m
of other liabilities, and 20m fully diluted units:

```{math}
NAV=141.18+4-60-3
=\text{MXN }82.18\text{m},
```

```{math}
NAV_{\text{per unit}}=\frac{82.18}{20}
=\text{MXN }4.11.
```

Cap-rate sensitivity is nonlinear:

| Cap rate | Property value | NAV per unit |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7.5% | MXN 160.00m | MXN 5.05 |
| 8.5% | MXN 141.18m | MXN 4.11 |
| 9.5% | MXN 126.32m | MXN 3.37 |

The case does not justify buying or selling at a particular market price. It
shows how occupancy, NOI, cap rates, leverage, and unit count must be linked.

### Mexican FIBRAs

FIBRAs provide listed access to real-estate trust interests in Mexico
{cite}`bmvFibras`. A reproducible analysis records the specific certificate,
property segments, occupancy convention, gross leasable area unit, NOI and
AFFO definitions, debt currency and maturity, distributions, unit count,
valuation date, and disclosure source. Exchange listing does not eliminate
property appraisal or refinancing risk.

## Infrastructure and natural resources

Infrastructure cash flows should be classified by revenue mechanism:

| Model | Primary driver | Key risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Availability payment | asset availability and service standard | counterparty and performance deductions |
| Regulated asset | approved asset base and tariff | regulatory reset and allowed return |
| Concession | traffic or volume times tariff | demand, tariff, renewal, political risk |
| Merchant asset | market price times output | price volatility, dispatch, curtailment |
| Contracted offtake | contracted volume and price | offtaker credit and contract expiry |

The valuation also records construction, completion, operating, maintenance,
financing, inflation-indexation, currency, environmental, community, and
residual-value assumptions.

Timberland and farmland combine:

- land-value change;
- biological growth or crop yield;
- commodity sale price;
- operating and harvesting cost;
- weather, disease, water, climate, and environmental risk;
- storage or harvest-timing optionality;
- long and uncertain transaction horizons.

A natural-resource return should not be described as pure inflation protection.
The result depends on local supply, global prices, input costs, currency,
leverage, and the purchase valuation.

## Commodities and roll mechanics

A futures-based commodity position can earn three conceptually separate
components:

```{math}
R_{\text{total}}
\approx R_{\text{spot}}
+R_{\text{roll}}
+R_{\text{collateral}},
```

before fees, taxes, and interaction effects. This is an approximate additive
attribution over short horizons.

- **Contango:** longer-dated futures prices exceed near-dated prices under the
  stated curve comparison.
- **Backwardation:** longer-dated futures prices are below near-dated prices.
- **Roll return:** gain or loss produced by replacing an expiring contract with
  another maturity and by convergence, separate from collateral income.

Suppose spot remains USD 100 through expiry:

| Initial futures purchase | Expiry value | Contract return |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| USD 102 in contango | USD 100 | \(100/102-1=-1.96\%\) |
| USD 98 in backwardation | USD 100 | \(100/98-1=2.04\%\) |

This isolates convergence and assumes no change in spot, margin cash flow,
transaction cost, collateral return, or FX. Actual roll return depends on the
contracts and dates selected.

Commodity swaps exchange a contractual commodity return or price for a fixed
or floating payment. Futures and swaps add margin, collateral, basis,
counterparty, and roll-schedule risk. Commodity indexes can differ because of
contract selection, weighting, concentration caps, roll dates, collateral
rules, and rebalancing. Cost-of-carry and derivative pricing foundations follow
the conventions introduced in the derivatives module {cite}`hull2022options`.

## Checks and limitations

- Label NOI as historical, current, or forward and state the period.
- Do not call FFO or AFFO comparable without a definition bridge.
- Reconcile fully diluted units and property-level versus corporate debt.
- Separate appraised NAV from traded price.
- State whether infrastructure revenue is demand-based, regulated, contracted,
  or merchant.
- For natural resources, identify unit of land, production, commodity price,
  and currency.
- For commodity exposure, report contract, exchange, multiplier, quote unit,
  expiry, roll rule, collateral treatment, and FX.
- The worked examples are simulations, not estimates of a particular FIBRA or
  commodity index.

## Practice

A simulated property has forward NOI of MXN 18m, debt of MXN 75m, other net
assets of MXN 5m, and 25m fully diluted units.

1. Calculate property value and NAV per unit at cap rates of 7.0%, 8.0%, and
   9.0%.
2. Explain why the 8.0% result is not automatically a fair listed-unit price.
3. A commodity future is purchased at USD 104 and converges to unchanged spot
   of USD 101. Calculate the contract return and label the source of that
   return.

```{dropdown} Suggested answer
Property values are MXN 257.14m, 225.00m, and 200.00m. After subtracting MXN
75m of debt and adding MXN 5m of other net assets, NAV per unit is MXN 7.49,
6.20, and 5.20. The listed price can differ because the estimate omits detailed
cash flows, corporate costs, taxes, management, financing, growth, governance,
liquidity, and appraisal uncertainty. The futures return is
\(101/104-1=-2.88\%\); under the unchanged-spot assumption it is a negative
convergence or roll component before collateral and costs.
```
