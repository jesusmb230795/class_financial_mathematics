# Private Wealth Pathway

Module: Advanced Pathways and Capstone

## Lesson summary

Private wealth decisions combine an investment portfolio with a person's
business ownership, human capital, family system, liabilities, taxes, spending,
philanthropy, and transfer objectives. The analytical challenge is not to
maximize a standalone portfolio ratio. It is to preserve decision capacity and
fund the family's goals under uncertainty, concentration, and changing
authority.

This lesson constructs a wealth balance sheet, a stressed liquidity plan, and a
governance map. It connects client management and wealth planning to measurable
resources and controls, consistent with a goals-based framework that includes
human capital, liabilities, taxation, and liquidity {cite}`cfaWealthPlanning2026`.
It is an educational framework, not individualized legal, tax, estate-planning,
or investment advice.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- distinguish major private-wealth service and custody arrangements by role,
  authority, conflict, and fee;
- convert client discovery into objectives, constraints, priorities, and
  decision rights;
- combine financial assets, liabilities, private business interests, and human
  capital in a scenario-based wealth balance sheet;
- build a liquidity plan for spending, taxes, commitments, collateral, and
  emergencies;
- analyze a concentrated position without assuming immediate sale;
- connect entrepreneurship, family dynamics, philanthropy, incapacity, and
  wealth transfer to portfolio governance;
- document where qualified legal, tax, insurance, or other specialist advice
  is required.

## Prerequisites and notation

Complete the [Module 10 overview](../../chapters/10-capstone.md), the Module 9

The lesson uses:

- \(F\): current financial capital;
- \(H\): scenario value of future after-tax labor or entrepreneurial income;
- \(P\): private-business and other illiquid interests;
- \(D\): liabilities and contractual obligations;
- \(N\): net family resources under the stated scenario;
- \(L_h\): assets available inside liquidity horizon \(h\);
- \(O_h\): required outflows inside horizon \(h\).

Currency, measurement date, ownership, tax status, liquidity, and valuation
status are stated for every balance.

## The wealth-management system

A family may interact with private banks, brokers, independent advisers,
single- or multi-family offices, asset managers, trustees, custodians,
accountants, attorneys, insurance specialists, and operating-company teams.
The wealth-management industry distributes advice, products, custody, and
administration across these institutions, so role and authority cannot be
inferred from one provider label.
The service map records:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Role | Advice, discretionary management, execution, custody, administration, tax, legal, or insurance? |
| Authority | Who can recommend, approve, execute, custody, and report? |
| Standard and scope | Which accounts, entities, jurisdictions, and decisions are covered? |
| Compensation | Asset-based, retainer, commission, spread, product fee, carry, or combination? |
| Conflict | Proprietary product, referral, affiliated custody, transaction incentive, or related party? |
| Continuity | Who acts during absence, incapacity, death, dispute, or provider failure? |

No provider should both define and validate its own performance, asset values,
and custody records without an independent reconciliation.

## Client discovery and family governance

Discovery separates **facts**, **preferences**, and **assumptions**:

- legal owners, entities, beneficiaries, liabilities, and decision authority;
- spending, taxes, capital calls, planned purchases, and contingent obligations;
- goals, timing, priority, currency, and acceptable flexibility;
- business ownership, employment, human capital, and family dependencies;
- risk capacity, risk tolerance, loss experience, and behavioral responses;
- philanthropic intent and transfer objectives;
- privacy, reporting, education, and family-participation preferences.

A family-governance map names who proposes, decides, advises, executes,
receives information, and resolves disputes. Silence by one family member is
not evidence of agreement. A plan should distinguish financial authority from
family influence and document consent for the information used.

## Wealth balance sheet and human capital

A scenario resource identity is:

```{math}
N=F+H+P-D.
```

Human capital can be approximated as the present value of future after-tax
income:

```{math}
H_0=\sum_{t=1}^{T}
\frac{\mathbb{E}[Y_t^{after\ tax}]}{(1+r_t+\lambda_t)^t}.
```

This is a scenario model, not a tradable asset value. Income may be
non-transferable, correlated with the family business, vulnerable to health or
employment shocks, and dependent on personal effort. A founder whose salary,
dividends, and net worth all depend on one company has more concentration than
a securities account alone reveals.

The balance sheet therefore labels:

- liquid versus illiquid;
- market price versus appraisal or model;
- personal versus entity ownership;
- pledged, restricted, or encumbered status;
- currency and jurisdiction;
- tax basis when authorized and relevant;
- exposure shared across business, property, employment, and portfolio.

## Liquidity planning

For horizon \(h\):

```{math}
Coverage_h=\frac{L_h}{O_h}.
```

\(O_h\) includes spending, taxes, debt service, capital calls, collateral,
insurance premiums, planned gifts, purchases, and a stress reserve. \(L_h\)
uses stressed values after sale time, tax, spread, market impact, borrowing
capacity limits, and legal restrictions. A credit line is contingent funding,
not cash; the plan states covenants, collateral, maturity, and renewal risk.

A useful liquidity ladder separates:

| Horizon | Purpose | Typical control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate to 12 months | operations, emergencies, collateral | cash and high-quality liquid reserve |
| 1 to 3 years | known taxes, tuition, commitments, purchases | matched low-volatility funding |
| 3 to 10 years | strategic goals and transition capital | diversified growth and liability plan |
| Beyond 10 years | legacy, philanthropy, multigenerational capital | governance and long-horizon allocation |

## Concentrated positions and entrepreneurs

A concentrated public or private position can carry tax, control, employment,
identity, voting, dividend, collateral, and family consequences. The decision
set may include:

- retain with explicit risk limits;
- staged sale or secondary transaction;
- diversify future savings and distributions;
- hedge part of the market exposure when legally and operationally feasible;
- recapitalize or borrow without assuming refinancing is guaranteed;
- donate under a reviewed philanthropic and tax plan;
- transfer interests under reviewed governance and valuation.

Each alternative is evaluated for cash flow, taxes, control, dilution,
counterparty, basis, liquidity, legal restrictions, cost, and failure modes.
No tax or legal outcome is assumed without qualified review.

## Worked family case

A simulated entrepreneur has:

| Resource | MXN m | Liquidity status |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Private operating company | 210 | illiquid, model value |
| Public securities | 30 | liquid, market value |
| Cash | 20 | immediate |
| Real estate | 40 | illiquid, appraisal |
| **Total** | **300** | |

The private company is 70% of reported wealth:

```{math}
\frac{210}{300}=70\%.
```

The family wants three years of MXN 12m annual spending plus MXN 18m of known
taxes and commitments:

```{math}
O_{3y}=3(12)+18=\text{MXN }54\text{m}.
```

At current marks, cash and public securities provide MXN 50m, a MXN 4m
shortfall. Under a 20% stress haircut to public securities:

```{math}
L_{3y}=20+30(1-0.20)=\text{MXN }44\text{m},
```

so the stressed shortfall is MXN 10m and coverage is:

```{math}
Coverage_{3y}=\frac{44}{54}=0.81\times.
```

The first decision is not "sell the company." It is to close the liquidity
gap and reduce the probability that a forced transaction determines the
company outcome. The plan could test a spending reserve, scheduled company
distributions supported by cash flow, partial secondary sale, staged public
portfolio changes, contingent credit, or revised timing. Each route has
different control, tax, cost, and execution risks.

## Philanthropy and wealth transfer

A planning brief identifies:

- donor or transferor objective and intended beneficiary;
- amount, asset, timing, liquidity, control, and reversibility;
- family participation and governance;
- valuation and ownership evidence;
- legal, tax, accounting, and reporting review;
- investment policy of any receiving vehicle;
- success measure beyond tax outcome.

Transfer planning also addresses incapacity, succession, key access, business
continuity, digital records, and the authority to act. A structure that is
efficient in a model but cannot be administered by the family is not complete.

## Checks and limitations

- Obtain authorization before using personal, family, legal, or tax data.
- Separate facts from preferences and analyst assumptions.
- Do not count the same business value in financial and human capital.
- Stress liquidity before relying on a concentrated position or credit line.
- Include taxes and transaction costs only when supported by reviewed rules
  and case facts.
- Treat appraisals and private-company values as estimates.
- Name specialist referrals instead of presenting investment analysis as legal,
  tax, estate, or insurance advice.
- The worked case has no liabilities and uses a simplified three-year
  liquidity horizon.

## Practice

A simulated family reports MXN 180m of total wealth: MXN 108m in a private
company, MXN 24m cash, MXN 28m public securities, and MXN 20m property. Its
two-year required outflows are MXN 46m. Apply a 25% stress haircut to public
securities and treat cash as fully available.

1. Calculate company concentration.
2. Calculate stressed two-year liquid resources and coverage.
3. State the liquidity surplus or shortfall.
4. Propose one immediate control and one longer-term concentration response,
   each with a failure condition.

```{dropdown} Suggested answer
Company concentration is \(108/180=60\%\). Stressed liquid resources are
\(24+28(0.75)=\) MXN 45m, so coverage is \(45/46=0.98\times\) and the shortfall
is MXN 1m. An immediate control could reserve an additional MXN 1m plus a
margin of safety before new illiquid commitments. A longer-term response could
diversify future distributions or evaluate a staged secondary sale; it fails
if company cash flow cannot support distributions or transaction terms destroy
control or value beyond the approved limit.
```
