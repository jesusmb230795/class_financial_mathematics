# Investment Thesis and Valuation Memo

Module: Corporate Issuers and Equity Valuation

## Lesson summary

An investment thesis is a falsifiable explanation of why future operating
results, risk, or capital allocation may differ from the expectations embedded
in price. A valuation memo records the evidence, assumptions, scenario range,
catalysts, risks, and monitoring plan so that the conclusion can be reviewed
without hindsight.

This lesson closes the module with a simulated equity research note. It is an
educational analytical record, not a recommendation to buy or sell a security.
Professional research may also require conflict disclosures, supervision, and
analyst certifications under the rules that apply to its jurisdiction and
distribution channel; U.S. Regulation AC is one concrete example, not a
universal rule for this classroom memo {cite}`sec2003RegulationAC`.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- distinguish a thesis from a company description or valuation output;
- connect evidence, variant view, operating driver, cash flow, and value;
- define catalysts without assuming price convergence;
- express risks as causal paths with observable indicators;
- build a scenario valuation range and avoid unsupported precision;
- design a monitoring dashboard with thresholds and review actions;
- write a concise equity research note with sources, limitations, and a
  falsification test.

## Prerequisites and notation

Complete [Equity Valuation Frameworks](5.4.equity_valuation_frameworks.md) and
the Module 4 [Three-Statement Model and Review Memo](4.5.three_statement_model_and_review.md).

Use an explicit as-of date, reporting currency, value per diluted share, and
scenario label. A catalyst is an event that may reveal information or change
cash flows; it is not a guarantee that market price reaches estimated value.

## Thesis architecture

A complete thesis follows this chain:

```text
source-backed fact
→ interpretation that differs from a stated market expectation
→ operating driver
→ financial statement and cash-flow effect
→ valuation consequence
→ observable catalyst or monitoring evidence.
```

For example:

```text
renewal data show improving retention
→ consensus-like steady churn is too conservative
→ average customers and contribution rise
→ revenue, EBIT, and FCFF exceed the base expectation
→ justified value increases
→ quarterly cohort disclosure confirms or rejects the path.
```

"The company is a market leader" is not a thesis. It lacks a measured
expectation, cash-flow bridge, and falsification condition. A research
conclusion needs a reasonable evidence base, documented assumptions, and
ongoing review rather than confidence alone {cite}`cfaStandardVA2024`.

## Research note structure

Use a two-layer output.

### First-read page

1. **Header:** issuer, identifier, currency, as-of date, market price, diluted
   shares, and source vintage.
2. **Conclusion:** one paragraph stating the variant view and valuation range.
3. **Three drivers:** evidence, forecast effect, and monitoring indicator.
4. **Valuation:** base, downside, and upside values with primary method.
5. **Catalysts and risks:** dated or condition-based items.
6. **What changes the view:** explicit thresholds and next review date.

### Supporting analysis

- business and industry map;
- normalized historical statements;
- forecast and scenario bridges;
- WACC and capital structure;
- DCF and cross-checks;
- source register, model checks, and limitations.

The first-read page should communicate the controlling evidence without
concealing uncertainty or overwhelming the reader with model detail
{cite}`knaflic2015storytelling`. Its narrative should remain connected to the
business model, resources, risks, performance, and cash-flow prospects disclosed
in the supporting analysis {cite}`ifrs2025ManagementCommentary`.

## Catalysts

A catalyst should specify:

- **event or condition:** filing, product launch, contract renewal, capacity
  ramp, refinancing, regulatory decision, asset sale, or capital return;
- **window:** date or observable threshold;
- **mechanism:** which uncertainty, cash flow, or claim changes;
- **expected evidence:** metric or disclosure that confirms the event;
- **failure state:** delay, partial delivery, adverse mix, or cost overrun.

Earnings publication by itself is not a thesis catalyst unless the memo states
which contested assumption the release can resolve.

## Risks and falsification

Write risks as causal chains:

```text
risk event
→ operating exposure
→ statement line
→ cash and financing effect
→ scenario value
→ observable warning.
```

Include:

- thesis-specific risk;
- balance-sheet and liquidity risk;
- industry, regulatory, technology, and macro risk;
- accounting, data, and model risk;
- governance and capital-allocation risk;
- valuation and terminal-assumption risk.

The falsification test should be stronger than "the stock price falls." Price
can move for reasons unrelated to the thesis. Use operating and financial
evidence such as retention, margin, cash conversion, leverage, or market share.

## Scenario valuation

Use internally coherent cases:

| Case | Operating state | Financing state | Valuation method | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Downside | Specific demand, margin, and reinvestment stress | Liquidity and spread response | DCF with stressed bridge | What can be lost under a plausible adverse path |
| Base | Most supported driver set | Target leverage and current funding evidence | Primary DCF plus cross-check | Central conditional estimate |
| Upside | Supported execution and competitive evidence | Funding consistent with growth | Same claim-consistent methods | Value if identified favorable drivers occur |

Do not average cases without supportable probabilities. A range communicates
conditional uncertainty; it should not be narrowed to create a visually precise
target.

If probabilities are defensible, disclose their source and compute an expected
value explicitly:

```{math}
\mathbb{E}[V]=\sum_{s=1}^{S}p_sV_s,
\qquad
p_s\ge 0,
\qquad
\sum_{s=1}^{S}p_s=1.
```

An equal-weight average silently asserts equal probabilities; it is not a
neutral substitute for evidence.

## Worked example: Northstar Components

Northstar Components is a fictional manufacturer. All values and facts below
are simulated. The note is dated 31 December 20X1, uses USD, and assumes 100m
diluted shares. Annual FCFF occurs at year-end. The DCF uses effective annual
USD rates on a nominal, inflation-inclusive basis; debt, cash, non-controlling
interests (NCI), and shares are measured on the valuation date.

The simulated claim and dilution bridge is:

| Item | Amount | Treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Basic common shares | 94m | Starting denominator |
| Restricted-share units | 3m | Included as dilutive awards |
| Incremental option shares | 2m | Simplified assumed-exercise teaching assumption |
| If-converted shares | 1m | Related convertible debt excluded from debt below |
| **Fully diluted shares** | **100m** | Denominator for every per-share value |
| Interest-bearing debt | USD 620m | Subtracted from enterprise value; excludes assumed conversion |
| Non-controlling interests (NCI) | USD 20m | Subtracted from enterprise value |
| Excess cash | USD 120m | Added to enterprise value |

```{math}
N_{\mathrm{diluted}}
=94+3+2+1
=100\text{m shares}.
```

The 2m option amount is already the net incremental share count after a
simplified treasury-stock-method offset; exercise proceeds are therefore not
added to excess cash again. The 1m if-converted shares replace the related
convertible claim, which is excluded from the USD 620m debt balance. Every DCF,
multiple, and residual-income amount below uses this same 100m denominator.

Net debt is therefore USD 500m. Simulated last-twelve-month EBITDA is USD
208.3m, so net debt/EBITDA is approximately 2.4×. The fully diluted denominator
illustrates claim-alignment discipline; it is not an IAS 33 or U.S. GAAP
calculation for a real issuer {cite}`ias33EarningsPerShare2003`.

The source register for this worked example is intentionally explicit:

| Input family | Provenance | As-of and review control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company, operating, claim, and scenario facts | Author-created simulation stated on this page; no issuer, market, or licensed third-party data | 31 December 20X1; model owner: course valuation analyst |
| Valuation mechanics | Equations in Lessons 5.2–5.4 and the cited primary or authoritative sources | Recalculate with each model change |
| Next review | New simulated quarter, refinancing evidence, or a threshold breach | 31 March 20X2, whichever occurs first |

This simulation does not close the editorial gap for a filing-backed case. A
future real-issuer source register should retain issuer, form, accession number,
filing and period dates, retrieval date, exact tables and notes used, and a
separate redistribution-rights review. SEC EDGAR and Inline XBRL provide
authoritative U.S. filing interfaces; IFRS 8 identifies segment disclosures
that can anchor a segment perimeter
{cite}`secEdgarApis2025,secInlineXbrl2025,ifrs8OperatingSegments2006`.

### Evidence and variant view

| Evidence | Base interpretation | Variant view to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue is USD 1,000m and EBIT margin is 12% | Mid-cycle margin remains near 12% | Contract repricing and a completed automation program can lift margin toward 15% |
| Customer concentration is high | Renewal risk offsets pricing | Multi-year renewals can reduce volume risk but do not remove bargaining power |
| ROIC is 11% | Value creation cannot be assessed until ROIC is compared with a risk-, tax-, currency-, and perimeter-consistent WACC | Better utilization and working capital can raise incremental ROIC |
| Net debt/EBITDA is 2.4× | Leverage may constrain flexibility; maturity, coverage, covenant, liquidity, and FCFF evidence determine severity | FCFF growth and refinancing can reduce leverage if distributions remain controlled |

### Driver bridge

The base thesis assumes:

- 6.11% three-year compound annual growth rate (CAGR), assuming each year
  repeats multiplicative 3% volume, 2% realized-price, and 1% mix effects:

  ```{math}
  (1.03)(1.02)(1.01)-1=6.1106\%.
  ```

- EBIT margin rises from 12% to 15% through utilization and automation, partly
  offset by wages and input costs;
- the cash conversion cycle improves by five days;
- capital expenditure remains above depreciation during the ramp;
- net debt/EBITDA falls below 2.0× before material discretionary payout.

The thesis is not "margin expands." It is that identified price, utilization,
cost, and working-capital changes produce higher normalized EBIT and FCFF
without requiring leverage to fund the result.

### Valuation range

The first DCF year reconciles the operating thesis to cash. Revenue grows by
6.1106%, EBIT margin reaches 13.0%, depreciation and amortization (D&A) is USD
90.00m, capital expenditure remains above D&A, and the cash-cycle improvement
releases USD 11.54m of operating working capital:

| Year 1 bridge | USD m |
| --- | ---: |
| Revenue | 1,061.11 |
| EBIT at 13.0% | 137.94 |
| D&A | 90.00 |
| EBITDA | 227.94 |
| NOPAT at 25.0% | 103.46 |
| Capital expenditure | (100.00) |
| Change in operating working capital | (11.54) |
| **FCFF** | **105.00** |

The working-capital amount is shown as a decrease in the balance. Under the
signed cash-flow convention:

```{math}
\mathrm{FCFF}_1
=103.46+90.00-100.00-(-11.54)
=105.00.
```

The base FCFF path is USD 105m, 130m, 155m, 180m, and 192m in Years 1–5.
At 9.0% WACC and 3.0% perpetual nominal growth:

```{math}
\mathrm{EV}_0
=\sum_{t=1}^{5}\frac{\mathrm{FCFF}_t}{1.09^t}
+\frac{192(1.03)}{(0.09-0.03)1.09^5}
=2{,}719.91.
```

```{math}
\text{Base value per diluted share}
=\frac{\mathrm{EV}-620-20+120}{100}
=22.00.
```

Holding the base FCFF path and 9.0% WACC fixed while moving perpetual growth
from 2.5% to 3.5% produces a local DCF sensitivity of USD 20.26–24.06 per
share. The broader scenario cases below change operating cash flow and WACC as
well.

For the hypothetical multiple cross-check, the same Year 1 bridge gives EBITDA
of USD 227.94m. A 10.7×–12.4× EV/EBITDA range gives:

```{math}
\mathrm{EBITDA}_1
=\mathrm{EBIT}_1+\mathrm{D\&A}_1
=137.944+90.000
=227.944\text{ USD m}.
```

```{math}
\frac{227.944(10.7)-520}{100}=19.19,
\qquad
\frac{227.944(12.4)-520}{100}=23.07.
```

For a stylized residual-income cross-check, beginning common book value is USD
12.00 per share, cost of equity is 10%, perpetual residual-income growth is 2%,
and next-period residual income is USD 0.64–0.80 per share:

```{math}
V_0
=12+\frac{\mathrm{RI}_1}{0.10-0.02}
=20.00\text{ to }22.00.
```

This accounting cross-check implies next-period common net income of USD
1.84–2.00 per share and beginning-equity ROE of 15.33%–16.67%:

```{math}
\mathrm{NI}_1^{\mathrm{common}}
=\mathrm{RI}_1+k_eB_0
=0.64\text{ to }0.80+0.10(12)
=1.84\text{ to }2.00.
```

On the common 100m-share basis, beginning common book value is USD 1,200m,
residual income is USD 64m–80m, and next-period common net income is USD
184m–200m:

```{math}
B_0=12(100)=1{,}200,
\qquad
\mathrm{NI}_1^{\mathrm{common}}
=64\text{ to }80+0.10(1{,}200)
=184\text{ to }200\text{ USD m}.
```

It becomes corroborating evidence only after the full common-income,
clean-surplus, and share-count schedules reconcile to the operating forecast.

| Method | USD/share range | Role |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| FCFF DCF | 20.3–24.1 | Primary operating valuation and local terminal-growth sensitivity |
| Forward EV/EBITDA | 19.2–23.1 | Hypothetical peer and expectation cross-check |
| Residual income | 20–22 | Accounting-return cross-check |
| **Triangulated base range** | **20–23** | Emphasizes DCF and overlap |

Assume the hypothetical market price is USD 18.00. The midpoint of the base range
is not a promised price target. It is a conditional estimate dependent on the
margin, working-capital, capital expenditure, leverage, WACC, and terminal
assumptions {cite}`damodaran2012investment`.

The triangulated range is a documented judgment, not an arithmetic average of
methods that share assumptions. The scenario outputs are fully reproducible
from the compact schedules below:

```{math}
\mathrm{EV}_{0,s}
=\sum_{t=1}^{5}\frac{\mathrm{FCFF}_{t,s}}{(1+\mathrm{WACC}_s)^t}
+\frac{\mathrm{FCFF}_{5,s}(1+g_s)}
{(\mathrm{WACC}_s-g_s)(1+\mathrm{WACC}_s)^5},
```

```{math}
V_{\mathrm{share},s}
=\frac{\mathrm{EV}_{0,s}-620-20+120}{100}.
```

| Case | FCFF, Years 1–5 (USD m) | WACC | Perpetual growth |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Downside | 92.0 / 113.9 / 135.8 / 157.8 / 168.3 | 10.0% | 2.5% |
| Base | 105.0 / 130.0 / 155.0 / 180.0 / 192.0 | 9.0% | 3.0% |
| Upside | 108.0 / 133.7 / 159.4 / 185.1 / 197.4 | 8.5% | 3.5% |

| Case | Enterprise value | Common equity value | Value/share | Main operating interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Downside | USD 1,920.26m | USD 1,400.26m | USD 14.00 | Weaker demand and margin, delayed cash conversion, wider refinancing spread |
| Base | USD 2,719.91m | USD 2,199.91m | USD 22.00 | 6.11% CAGR, 15% terminal EBIT margin, five-day cash-cycle improvement |
| Upside | USD 3,320.25m | USD 2,800.25m | USD 28.00 | Faster volume ramp, 16% margin, disciplined reinvestment, leverage below target |

### Catalysts

1. **Contract renewals:** disclosed realized price and retained volume support or
   reject pricing power.
2. **Automation ramp:** quarterly utilization and unit cost show whether margin
   improvement is operational rather than accounting.
3. **Refinancing:** maturity extension and spread determine interest, liquidity,
   and payout capacity.
4. **Working-capital release:** receivable and inventory days demonstrate
   whether earnings convert to cash.

### Risks and monitoring

| Risk | Leading indicator | Threshold | Review action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customers reject price | Renewal volume and churn | Retention below 90% | Reduce revenue and margin case |
| Input cost offsets automation | Gross margin and unit cost | Gross margin below plan by 200 basis points for two periods | Rebuild cost curve |
| Inventory accumulates | Days inventory outstanding (DIO) and order intake | DIO above 100 days with falling orders | Stress cash and obsolescence |
| Refinancing weakens | Indicative spread and coverage | Interest coverage below 3.0× | Rebuild debt schedule and payout |
| Capital program overruns | Capex-to-budget and commissioning | Cost above budget by 15% or delay above one quarter | Lower ROIC and delay cash flow |

The view is falsified if retention remains below 90%, margin improvement cannot
be traced to operating evidence, or leverage fails to decline despite the
forecasted cash generation.

## Monitoring dashboard

Organize indicators by causal layer:

Days sales outstanding (DSO), days inventory outstanding (DIO), and days
payables outstanding (DPO) must use the same period and day-count basis. With
$d$ equal to 365, 360, or the actual days in the period, two useful diagnostics
are:

```{math}
\mathrm{CCC}=\mathrm{DSO}+\mathrm{DIO}-\mathrm{DPO},
```

```{math}
\mathrm{OWC}
\approx\frac{\mathrm{Revenue}}{d}\mathrm{DSO}
+\frac{\mathrm{COGS}}{d}(\mathrm{DIO}-\mathrm{DPO}).
```

Here COGS means cost of goods sold. The second expression is an approximation:
operating working capital (OWC) also depends on seasonality, taxes, contract
liabilities, other operating items, and the revenue/cost perimeter. A five-day
cash-conversion-cycle (CCC) change does not determine a cash release until the
affected base is stated.

| Layer | Indicator | Unit and frequency | Source | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Demand | Orders, backlog, retention, volume | Units or %, monthly/quarterly | Filing or operating disclosure | Revenue case |
| Price and mix | Realized price, discount, product mix | Currency/unit and %, quarterly | Segment disclosure | Pricing power |
| Operations | Utilization, yield, unit cost, capacity | %, units, currency/unit | Operating report | Margin and capex |
| Cash | Days sales outstanding (DSO), DIO, days payables outstanding (DPO), FCFF | Days and currency, quarterly | Statements and notes | Liquidity |
| Financing | Net leverage, coverage, maturity, spread | Multiple, date, basis points | Debt note and market evidence | Capital structure |
| Value | WACC, terminal growth, peer multiple | %, ×, as-of date | Valuation snapshot | Range review |

Each indicator needs an owner, update date, source link, threshold, and action.
A dashboard without a decision rule is decoration. Independent validation,
ongoing monitoring, documented limitations, and governance are useful model-
risk disciplines. The 2026 U.S. interagency guidance provides a rigorous
benchmark, but its formal supervisory scope is banking and it is not imposed on
this educational equity memo {cite}`fed2026ModelRiskGuidance`.

## Memo quality controls

- The as-of date and sources are visible.
- Facts, management claims, market observations, and analyst estimates are
  separated.
- The thesis fits in one causal paragraph.
- Forecast, valuation, and catalyst refer to the same drivers.
- The downside affects statements, cash, financing, and value.
- Per-share values use the same diluted share count and claim bridge.
- Risks include evidence that can falsify the thesis.
- The next review date and model owner are named.
- Language states uncertainty; it does not imply guaranteed convergence.
- Any professional conflict disclosure or analyst certification required by the
  applicable regime is completed outside this classroom template
  {cite}`sec2003RegulationAC`.

## Checks and limitations

- A catalyst can reveal adverse information.
- A valuation gap can persist or reflect model error.
- Monitoring indicators can be revised, delayed, or selectively disclosed.
- Management guidance is evidence, not a substitute for independent modeling.
- A concise memo can conceal model complexity; retain supporting schedules and
  source traces.
- This educational template does not address suitability, portfolio
  constraints, legal disclosure duties, or investment-advisory requirements.

## Practice

Prepare a one-page note for a simulated issuer with:

- hypothetical market price of USD 25 per share;
- downside, base, and upside values of USD 17, USD 30, and USD 39;
- forecast revenue growth of 5%, EBIT-margin improvement from 9% to 12%, and
  net debt/EBITDA reduction from 3.0× to 2.2×;
- customer concentration of 35%, a major plant ramp, and refinancing due in 18
  months.

Your note must include:

1. a one-sentence variant view;
2. a driver-to-FCFF bridge;
3. two catalysts;
4. three risks with indicators and thresholds;
5. a valuation-method hierarchy;
6. a falsification test and review date.

```{dropdown} Review standard
A strong response does not merely restate the values. It explains how revenue,
margin, reinvestment, working capital, and interest produce the base value;
tests customer loss, plant delay, and refinancing in the downside; identifies
observable catalyst evidence; and states an operating or financing threshold
that would invalidate the thesis. The current price, valuation date, currency,
diluted shares, and source vintage must be visible.
```
