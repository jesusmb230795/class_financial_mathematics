# Private-Company Valuation and Due Diligence

Module: Alternative Investments

## Lesson summary

Private-company valuation is an evidence range, not a continuously observed
price. Forecasts, comparable-company multiples, transaction evidence, capital
structure, contractual rights, and dilution can each change the value received
by an investor. Due diligence tests whether those inputs and the manager's
ability to act on them are credible.

This lesson combines a valuation bridge with a manager-and-deal diligence log.
The objective is not to manufacture precision. It is to make the source,
assumption, ownership claim, and sensitivity behind every reported value
auditable {cite}`damodaran2012investment`.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- define a private-company valuation date, ownership claim, currency, and unit;
- triangulate discounted cash flow, trading-comparable, and transaction
  evidence without averaging incompatible values;
- bridge enterprise value to equity value and the value of a specific stake;
- assess forecast, multiple, leverage, dilution, and exit sensitivity;
- distinguish investment, operational, legal, tax, and manager due diligence;
- evaluate track records while recognizing selection, survivorship, valuation,
  and cash-flow-timing biases.

## Prerequisites and notation

Complete [Private Capital Structures and Fund Economics](8.1.private_capital_structures_and_economics.md).
This lesson uses:

- \(EV\): enterprise value;
- \(E\): total equity value;
- \(D\): interest-bearing debt;
- \(O\): other debt-like claims not already included in \(D\);
- \(C\): unrestricted cash not required for operations;
- \(A_{NO}\): other non-operating assets not already included in \(C\);
- \(s\): fully diluted ownership share;
- \(FCFF_t\): nominal free cash flow to the firm in period \(t\);
- \(WACC_j\): effective one-period weighted-average cost of capital from period
  \(j-1\) to period \(j\), stated under the same currency and inflation basis as
  \(FCFF_t\).

Values are MXN millions at one valuation date unless stated otherwise.

## Define the claim before choosing a method

A valuation header records:

| Field | Required answer |
| --- | --- |
| Subject | legal entity, operating perimeter, and security class |
| Date | valuation date and financial-statement vintage |
| Claim | enterprise, common equity, preferred equity, debt, or specific units |
| Currency and unit | transaction currency, reporting currency, FX convention, display scale |
| Ownership | issued and fully diluted shares, options, convertibles, preferences, and \(s\) |
| Purpose | transaction, monitoring, financial reporting, collateral, or portfolio decision |
| Standard | market participant, investment value, liquidation, or other declared basis |

A controlling stake, minority common share, preferred security, and employee
option do not have identical claims even when they reference the same company.

## Valuation methods

### Discounted cash flow

```{math}
EV_0=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{FCFF_t}{\prod_{j=1}^{t}(1+WACC_j)}
+\frac{TV_T}{\prod_{j=1}^{T}(1+WACC_j)}.
```

The analyst states forecast period, nominal or real cash flows, currency,
compounding, terminal method, capital structure, and whether scenario
probabilities are incorporated in cash flows or discount rates. A private
company may require explicit customer concentration, key-person, funding,
governance, and liquidity scenarios. Adding an arbitrary "private-company
discount" without identifying the underlying risk can double-count uncertainty
already modeled in cash flows or the discount rate.
This discount-factor form remains coherent when the required return varies by
period because each \(WACC_j\) is an effective sequential holding-period rate:

```{math}
D(0,t)=\prod_{j=1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+WACC_j}.
```

With a constant WACC it reduces to the familiar $(1+WACC)^t$ denominator. If
the available inputs are spot rates by maturity rather than sequential
one-period rates, use their directly implied discount factors instead of
multiplying the spot rates {cite}`damodaran2012investment`.

When scenarios are mutually exclusive and probabilities are defensible, keep
the probability weighting visible:

```{math}
EV_0=\sum_{s=1}^{S}p_sEV_{0,s},
\qquad p_s\geq0,
\qquad \sum_{s=1}^{S}p_s=1.
```

Do not also add a discount-rate premium for the same scenario risk.

### Trading comparables

Comparable-company analysis applies a consistently defined multiple to a
consistently defined metric:

```{math}
EV=M_{EV/EBITDA}\times EBITDA.
```

The peer set should be screened for business model, geography, growth,
profitability, accounting policy, cyclicality, scale, and capital intensity.
An observed public multiple includes market liquidity and current expectations;
it is not automatically transferable to a private minority stake. Peer
selection and multiple definitions materially affect the estimate
{cite}`liuNissimThomas2002Multiples,bhojrajLee2002Peer`.

### Precedent transactions

Transaction multiples can reflect control, expected synergies, financing
conditions, and a different market regime. Record announcement and completion
dates, consideration, assumed debt, contingent payments, metric vintage, and
whether the multiple includes a control premium.
Transaction and market multiples remain valuation evidence only after the
numerator, denominator, ownership claim, and measurement date are reconciled
{cite}`damodaran2012investment`.

### Scenario and milestone methods

Early-stage companies may lack stable cash flow or comparable metrics.
Milestone scenarios can make survival, follow-on funding, dilution, and exit
assumptions explicit. The result remains a model estimate, not an observed
price.

## Enterprise-to-equity bridge

A basic bridge is:

```{math}
E=EV-D-O+C+A_{NO}.
```

Other debt-like claims \(O\) can include unpaid consideration, pension deficits,
preferred claims, or other items justified by the analytical purpose. The
definitions are mutually exclusive: do not include the same liability in both
\(D\) and \(O\), the same asset in both \(C\) and \(A_{NO}\), or any bridge item
already captured in the cash-flow forecast.

For a simple common-equity claim:

```{math}
V_{\text{stake}}=sE.
```

Preferred rights, liquidation preferences, anti-dilution clauses, options,
convertibles, and control rights can invalidate that proportional allocation.
A cap table and the governing agreements are required.

## Worked example: multiple and capital-structure sensitivity

A simulated services company reports normalized next-twelve-month EBITDA of
MXN 8m. At the valuation date it has MXN 24m of interest-bearing debt and MXN
4m of unrestricted cash. Assume no other bridge items and a 20% fully diluted
common-equity stake.

At an \(8.0\times\) EV/EBITDA multiple:

```{math}
EV=8.0\times8=\text{MXN }64\text{m},
```

```{math}
E=64-24+4=\text{MXN }44\text{m},
```

```{math}
V_{\text{stake}}=0.20\times44=\text{MXN }8.8\text{m}.
```

Sensitivity to the selected multiple is:

| EV/EBITDA | Enterprise value | Equity value | 20% stake |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(6.5\times\) | MXN 52.0m | MXN 32.0m | MXN 6.4m |
| \(8.0\times\) | MXN 64.0m | MXN 44.0m | MXN 8.8m |
| \(9.5\times\) | MXN 76.0m | MXN 56.0m | MXN 11.2m |

The stake value changes by 75% from the low to high case even though EBITDA is
fixed. A decision memo must therefore defend the peer set and show operating
and leverage sensitivity, not only a point estimate.

## Due diligence architecture

### Deal diligence

| Workstream | Evidence | Failure question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial | customer cohorts, retention, price/volume, market structure | What must be true for revenue to persist? |
| Financial | filings, ledgers, quality of earnings, working capital, debt | Which reported earnings fail to convert to cash? |
| Operational | capacity, systems, suppliers, people, cyber controls | What prevents the plan from being executed? |
| Legal and tax | ownership, contracts, litigation, licenses, tax exposures | Does the investor own the modeled claim? |
| Governance | board rights, reserved matters, reporting, related parties | Who can change cash flows or exit timing? |
| Sustainability and externalities | permits, resource use, labor, community impact | Which obligations can alter value or license to operate? |
| Exit and liquidity | buyer set, transfer rights, refinancing, market depth | How and when can proceeds be realized? |

Each finding has an owner, source, date, severity, valuation effect, mitigation,
and unresolved status. "No issue identified" is not evidence unless the scope
and documents reviewed are recorded.

### Manager selection

Manager review covers:

- team stability, decision rights, succession, key-person provisions, and
  personal capital at risk;
- strategy capacity, sourcing evidence, repeatability, sector and geographic
  concentration;
- track-record attribution by deal, partner, vintage, gross/net status, and
  realized/unrealized split;
- valuation governance, external review, audit, conflicts, allocation policy,
  fee offsets, and related-party transactions;
- fund administration, cash controls, cybersecurity, compliance, business
  continuity, and service providers;
- portfolio monitoring, covenant handling, follow-on reserves, exit discipline,
  and reporting lag.

Due diligence must be documented at the depth warranted by the investment's
strategy and risks; relying on a manager's reputation or third-party
introduction does not replace review of operations, valuation, custody, and
conflicts {cite}`secAlternativeDueDiligence2014`.
Agency conflicts also make decision rights, related-party transactions, fee
allocation, and monitoring economically material rather than merely legal
appendices {cite}`jensenMeckling1976TheoryFirm`.

## Benchmarking challenges

Private-market comparisons are vulnerable to:

- self-selection and survivorship in databases;
- appraisal smoothing and stale valuations;
- vintage, geography, sector, currency, and strategy mismatch;
- different leverage and subscription-line use;
- gross versus net and fund-level versus deal-level reporting;
- interim IRRs dominated by residual NAV;
- public benchmarks that do not reproduce the fund's dated cash flows.

The comparison table should therefore include vintage, strategy, geography,
currency, leverage, fee status, realized share, measurement date, and benchmark
construction. Ranking a manager without these controls is not a valid
conclusion.
Judgmental peer and scenario selection should also be documented before the
result is known to reduce hindsight, anchoring, and availability bias
{cite}`tverskyKahneman1974Heuristics`.

## Checks and limitations

- Do not average DCF and multiple values unless the weights have a decision
  rationale.
- Reconcile enterprise and equity claims before applying ownership.
- Use fully diluted ownership and model preference rights when material.
- Preserve the historical financial vintage used in each valuation.
- Separate management case, underwriting case, and stress case.
- Record which diligence items remain unverified.
- A manager-provided NAV is reported evidence, not independent price
  discovery.
- The worked example excludes taxes, debt-like claims, dilution, control,
  transaction costs, and FX.

## Practice

A simulated company has normalized EBITDA of MXN 14m, debt of MXN 38m, cash
of MXN 6m, and 12% fully diluted common ownership for the investor.

1. Calculate the stake value at \(7.0\times\), \(8.0\times\), and
   \(9.0\times\) EV/EBITDA.
2. Identify one operating assumption and one legal term that could invalidate
   the proportional stake calculation.
3. Write one diligence request that would test each issue.

```{dropdown} Suggested answer
Enterprise values are MXN 98m, 112m, and 126m. Equity values after debt and
cash are MXN 66m, 80m, and 94m. The 12% stake values are therefore MXN 7.92m,
9.60m, and 11.28m. An operating issue could be whether EBITDA includes
non-recurring customer revenue; request a customer-level bridge from reported
to normalized EBITDA. A legal issue could be a preferred liquidation claim;
request the current cap table, shareholder agreement, and preference waterfall
and recalculate proceeds by security class.
```
