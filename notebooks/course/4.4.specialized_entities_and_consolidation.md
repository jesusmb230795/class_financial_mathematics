# Specialized Entities and Consolidation

Module: Financial Statement Analysis and Financial Modeling

## Lesson summary

The generic industrial-company model is not universal. For a bank, debt and
cash-like instruments are part of operations. For an insurer, reserve adequacy
and underwriting economics dominate a conventional working-capital analysis.
For a multinational group, currency translation and legal-entity boundaries
can obscure operating change. Acquisitions, noncontrolling interests, and
special-purpose entities also change which assets, liabilities, income, and
cash flows belong in the consolidated analysis.

This lesson preserves one principle across these cases: define the economic
claim, accounting perimeter, and cash-flow mechanism before selecting a ratio
or valuation method.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- adapt statement analysis for banks and insurers;
- distinguish transaction-currency effects from financial-statement
  translation;
- calculate a constant-currency revenue comparison with an explicit FX quote;
- distinguish passive investments, equity-method investments, consolidated
  subsidiaries, and noncontrolling interests;
- explain how purchase accounting, goodwill, and pro forma information affect
  acquisition analysis;
- identify control, guarantees, liquidity support, and off-balance-sheet risks
  associated with special-purpose entities.

## Prerequisites and notation

Complete [Accounting Quality and Normalization](4.3.accounting_quality_and_normalization.md)
and [Currency, Parity, and FX](3.3.currency_parity_fx.md).

For FX examples, $S_t$ is stated as reporting currency per unit of local
currency. $NCI$ denotes noncontrolling interest. The cited sections use IFRS
concepts; regulatory ratios and analytical measures remain
jurisdiction-specific and are not themselves IFRS-defined metrics. All
numerical examples are simulations and do not represent actual institutions.

## Banks: the balance sheet is the operating model

A bank raises funds through deposits, wholesale borrowing, and equity, then
holds loans, securities, reserves, and other financial assets. Interest-bearing
assets and liabilities are therefore operating items, not a financing layer
that can be removed mechanically.

An introductory bank map includes:

| Area | Evidence | Core questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net interest income | Interest income, interest expense, earning assets, funding | Are asset yields, funding costs, and repricing gaps sustainable? |
| Credit | Loan mix, delinquency, non-performing exposure, provisions, reserves, charge-offs | Are losses recognized promptly and reserves adequate for the scenario? |
| Fees and trading | Revenue mix, client flows, valuation inputs | How recurring, volatile, and market-sensitive are non-interest revenues? |
| Costs | Personnel, technology, branches, regulatory and remediation costs | Is operating leverage structural or temporary? |
| Capital | Risk-weighted assets, regulatory ratios, dividends, buybacks | What constrains balance-sheet growth and distributions? |
| Liquidity | Deposit behavior, liquid assets, collateral, wholesale maturities | Can the institution withstand funding outflows and margin calls? |

Useful measures include:

```{math}
\text{Net interest margin}
=\frac{\text{Net interest income}}
{\text{Average interest-earning assets}},
```

```{math}
\text{Cost of risk}
=\frac{\text{Credit-loss provision}}
{\text{Average gross loans}}.
```

For a $d$-day interim period annualized on a declared $D$-day basis:

```{math}
\text{Annualized net interest margin}
=\frac{\text{Period net interest income}}
{\text{Average interest-earning assets}}
\times\frac{D}{d}.
```

The exact denominator, day basis, annualization, and loan perimeter must be
disclosed.
Regulatory capital and liquidity measures follow jurisdiction-specific rules;
an analyst should use the controlling regulatory definition, not recreate it
from incomplete public fields. Traditional FCFF and net-debt/EBITDA are usually
poor primary measures for a bank because deposits, debt, liquidity, and
interest are intertwined with operations {cite}`mishkin2019financial`.

## Insurers: underwriting plus invested assets

An insurer receives premiums, pays claims and expenses, holds technical
reserves, and invests assets backing policyholder obligations and capital.
Property-and-casualty and life insurers have different claim durations and
risks, so their measures should not be mixed mechanically.
Insurance-contract recognition, measurement, presentation, and disclosure also
depend on the applicable framework {cite}`ifrs17InsuranceContracts2017`.

For a property-and-casualty insurer:

```{math}
\text{Loss ratio}
=\frac{\text{Claims and loss-adjustment expense}}
{\text{Earned premiums}},
```

```{math}
\text{Expense ratio}
=\frac{\text{Underwriting expenses}}
{\text{Earned premiums}},
```

```{math}
\text{Combined ratio}
=\text{Loss ratio}+\text{Expense ratio}.
```

A combined ratio below 100% indicates an underwriting profit before investment
income under the stated definition. It does not prove total profitability or
reserve adequacy. Analyze reserve development, catastrophe exposure,
reinsurance recoverability, asset-liability duration, credit quality,
liquidity, and regulatory solvency alongside the ratio.

For life insurance, lapse behavior, mortality or longevity assumptions,
guarantees, policyholder options, spread income, and the duration of liabilities
are central. A small change in a long-dated assumption can have a material
present-value effect.

## Multinational operations

### Transaction versus translation

A transaction exposure arises when a receivable, payable, debt, or commitment
is denominated in a currency different from the entity's functional currency.
Settlement creates a cash effect.

Translation converts foreign operations into the group's reporting currency.
It can change reported revenue, assets, liabilities, and equity without a
matching current-period cash flow. Record:

- functional currency of each material operation;
- reporting currency and translation method;
- average rate used for period flows and closing rate for balances;
- hedges and where their effects are reported;
- economies subject to exceptional inflation or capital controls;
- tax and dividend-remittance constraints.

### Worked example: constant-currency growth

A foreign operation reports local-currency revenue of LC 100m in Year 0 and LC
110m in Year 1. The reporting currency is USD. The rate is USD per LC:

| Measure | Year 0 | Year 1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Local-currency revenue | LC 100m | LC 110m |
| Average FX rate | 0.050 USD/LC | 0.045 USD/LC |
| Reported USD revenue | USD 5.00m | USD 4.95m |

Reported USD growth is:

```{math}
\begin{aligned}
g_{\mathrm{reported}}
&=\frac{R_{1,\mathrm{LC}}S_1}{R_{0,\mathrm{LC}}S_0}-1\\
&=\frac{110(0.045)}{100(0.050)}-1\\
&=-1.0\%.
\end{aligned}
```

At the Year 0 rate, Year 1 revenue would be:

```{math}
110\times0.050=5.50\text{ USD m},
```

and:

```{math}
\begin{aligned}
g_{\mathrm{constant\ currency}}
&=\frac{R_{1,\mathrm{LC}}S_0}{R_{0,\mathrm{LC}}S_0}-1\\
&=\frac{110}{100}-1\\
&=10.0\%.
\end{aligned}
```

Constant-currency analysis isolates one translation effect; it does not remove
transaction exposure, inflation, pricing, volume, hedging, or competitive
effects {cite}`ias21ForeignExchange2003`.

## Investments, control, and consolidation

The accounting and analytical treatment depends on economic influence and
control:

| Relationship | Typical presentation | Analytical focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passive financial interest | Investment asset measured at amortized cost, FVOCI, or FVTPL as applicable | Market value, income classification, liquidity, and tax |
| Significant influence without control | One-line investment and share of earnings under an equity method | Underlying operating results, distributions, debt, and hidden leverage |
| Controlled subsidiary | Line-by-line consolidation | Eliminate intercompany balances and transactions; identify NCI |
| Joint arrangement | Classification depends on rights and obligations | Proportionate economics, commitments, guarantees, and cash access |

Do not treat equity-method earnings as cash received. Reconcile share of profit
to dividends and assess whether the investee can distribute cash. Significant
influence, the equity method, control, and consolidation must follow the
applicable accounting perimeter. Under IFRS, IFRS 9 owns financial-instrument
classification, IAS 28 owns significant influence and the equity method, IFRS
10 owns control and consolidation, and IFRS 11 classifies joint arrangements
from the parties' rights and obligations
{cite}`ifrs9FinancialInstruments2014,ias28Associates2011,ifrs10Consolidated2011,ifrs11JointArrangements2011`.

### Worked example: consolidation eliminations

A parent owns 80% of a subsidiary. Before consolidation entries:

- parent revenue is USD 800m and EBIT is USD 120m;
- subsidiary revenue is USD 200m and EBIT is USD 30m;
- the subsidiary sold inventory to the parent for USD 50m;
- USD 10m of the intercompany profit remains in ending inventory.

Consolidated revenue and EBIT are:

```{math}
\text{Revenue}=800+200-50=950,
```

```{math}
\text{EBIT}=120+30-10=140.
```

If subsidiary net income before the elimination is USD 21m, the after-tax
effect of the inventory-profit elimination is USD 7.5m at a 25% tax rate, and
the elimination is attributable to the selling subsidiary, then the
subsidiary profit used for NCI is USD 13.5m. NCI income is:

```{math}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Adjusted subsidiary income}
&=21-10(1-0.25)=13.5,\\
\text{NCI income}
&=(1-0.80)(13.5)=2.7\text{ USD m}.
\end{aligned}
```

Ownership percentage alone is not enough. The analyst must identify which
entity generated an eliminated profit and whether contracts allocate economics
differently.

## Business combinations

An acquisition can change nearly every trend:

- acquired assets and liabilities are measured and consolidated from an
  effective date;
- intangible assets, deferred taxes, goodwill, and contingent consideration
  may be recognized;
- financing changes interest expense and share count;
- transaction and integration costs can be classified separately;
- seller accounting policies and fiscal periods require alignment;
- reported growth combines organic change with acquired activity.

Build three bridges:

1. **Purchase bridge:** consideration transferred to identifiable net assets,
   deferred taxes, NCI, and goodwill.
2. **Performance bridge:** reported revenue and earnings to a comparable pro
   forma period as if the perimeter were consistent.
3. **Cash bridge:** purchase price, acquired cash, fees, debt raised, shares
   issued, contingent payments, and integration investment.

When noncontrolling interest is measured at fair value, the full-goodwill
acquisition-method check is:

```{math}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Goodwill}
={}&\text{Consideration transferred}
+\mathrm{FV}(\mathrm{NCI})\\
&+\mathrm{FV}(\text{Previously held interest})
-\mathrm{FV}(\text{Identifiable net assets acquired}).
\end{aligned}
```

IFRS 3 also permits eligible NCI components to be measured at their
proportionate share of the acquiree's identifiable net assets. That
transaction-level choice produces partial rather than full goodwill; replace
the fair-value NCI term with the NCI amount measured under the selected policy.
For the 80% acquisition in the worked case below, proportionate-share NCI is
$0.20(170)=34$ USD m. The two policies therefore reconcile as:

```{math}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Partial goodwill}&=160+34-170=24,\\
\text{Full goodwill}&=160+40-170=30.
\end{aligned}
```

Goodwill is not a free operating asset. It represents residual acquisition
economics and must be tested against the forecast assumptions that justified
the purchase. ROIC should be shown both including and, only when analytically
useful, excluding goodwill—with both versions clearly labeled
{cite}`ifrs3BusinessCombinations2008`.

## Special-purpose entities and off-balance-sheet risk

A special-purpose entity can isolate assets, funding, risks, or contractual
rights. Legal form does not determine economic exposure. Ask:

- who controls the relevant decisions;
- who absorbs variable returns or losses;
- who provides guarantees, liquidity facilities, servicing, or collateral;
- whether assets can be accessed by the sponsor or its creditors;
- whether the entity creates purchase commitments or reputational support;
- what events trigger consolidation, collateral calls, or accelerated funding.

Map the maximum contractual exposure and a realistic stress exposure even when
the entity is not consolidated. Notes on securitizations, receivable programs,
supplier finance, guarantees, and unconsolidated vehicles can be more important
than the face of the statements. The accounting conclusion and the economic
risk map are related controls, not substitutes for one another
{cite}`ifrs10Consolidated2011`.

## Checks and limitations

- Regulatory ratios and accounting frameworks are jurisdiction-specific.
- Bank and insurer disclosures can use internal model inputs unavailable to an
  outside analyst.
- Constant-currency measures are management or analyst constructs and need a
  reproducible rate and perimeter.
- Pro forma acquisition data are not audited forecasts and can omit integration
  difficulties.
- NCI percentages do not always equal economic rights because of preferred
  claims, options, or contractual arrangements.
- Lack of consolidation does not imply lack of risk.

## Practice

1. Explain why net debt/EBITDA is not a primary solvency measure for a
   deposit-funded bank.
2. An insurer reports earned premiums of USD 500m, claims and loss-adjustment
   expense of USD 310m, and underwriting expenses of USD 125m. Calculate the
   loss, expense, and combined ratios.
3. A foreign subsidiary's revenue rises from LC 200m to LC 230m. The reporting
   rate moves from 0.060 to 0.050 USD/LC. Calculate reported USD growth and
   constant-currency growth at the prior rate.
4. Name two exposures that can remain even when a special-purpose entity is not
   consolidated.
5. A parent owns 75% of a subsidiary. Parent and subsidiary revenue are USD
   600m and USD 150m; EBIT is USD 90m and USD 24m. The subsidiary sold USD 30m
   of goods to the parent, with USD 6m of intercompany profit still in ending
   inventory. Subsidiary net income before elimination is USD 18m and the tax
   rate is 25%. Calculate consolidated revenue, consolidated EBIT, adjusted
   subsidiary income, and NCI income.
6. Consideration transferred is USD 160m, fair value of NCI is USD 40m, there
   is no previously held interest, and fair value of identifiable net assets is
   USD 170m. Calculate goodwill and name one disclosure needed to assess it.

```{dropdown} Suggested answer
Deposits and interest-bearing assets are operating inputs for a bank, so
subtracting cash and treating funding interest as a generic financing layer
misstates its business model. The insurer's loss ratio is 62%, expense ratio is
25%, and combined ratio is 87%. Foreign revenue moves from USD 12.0m to USD
11.5m, a reported decline of 4.17%; at the prior rate, Year 1 revenue is USD
13.8m, so constant-currency growth is 15.0%. Guarantees, liquidity facilities,
servicing duties, purchase commitments, reputational support, or collateral
calls can remain outside consolidation.

In the consolidation case, revenue is $600+150-30=720$ USD m and EBIT is
$90+24-6=108$ USD m. Adjusted subsidiary income is
$18-6(1-0.25)=13.5$ USD m, so NCI income is
$0.25(13.5)=3.375$ USD m, or USD 3.38m when rounded to two decimals.
Goodwill is $160+40-170=30$ USD m. Relevant evidence includes the valuation
of consideration and NCI, the identifiable-asset and liability fair-value
schedule, useful lives, deferred-tax effects, or the assumptions supporting
the acquired business.
```
