# Macro Scenarios and Capital Market Expectations

Module: Economics, Macro, and Currency

## Lesson summary

Capital market expectations translate macro views into assumptions about returns, volatility, correlations, yields, spreads, currencies, and risk premia. This lesson defines a concise scenario structure that can be used before valuation, risk, fixed-income, derivatives, or portfolio modules {cite}`damodaran2012investment,mishkin2019financial`.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- distinguish a base case from an active view;
- define scenarios with explicit macro assumptions;
- map macro shocks into cash-flow, discount-rate, risk-premium, and currency channels;
- interpret macro surprises without overfitting a single release;
- design dashboard requirements for macro monitoring;
- communicate uncertainty and limitations in an investment memo.

## Scenario template

Every macro scenario should fit on one page before any dashboard or model is built.

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Scenario name | Short label such as soft landing, sticky inflation, recession, fiscal stress, or currency pressure. |
| As-of date and horizon | Information cutoff, forecast horizon, and relevant market calendar. |
| Trigger | Data release, policy decision, external shock, liquidity event, or expectation change. |
| Macro assumptions | Growth, inflation, policy rate, fiscal stance, exchange rate, financial conditions, and global risk appetite. |
| Base and active view | Benchmark or consensus base case plus the explicit deviation being asserted. |
| Probability treatment | Assigned probability with a documented method, or an explicit statement that no probability is assigned. |
| Transmission channel | Cash flows, discount rates, risk premia, credit spreads, currency conversion, liquidity, or margin pressure. |
| Affected assets | Equities, fixed income, FX, commodities, real estate, credit, or alternatives. |
| Monitoring indicators | Series, source, frequency, release lag, threshold, and dashboard owner. |
| Uncertainty | Alternative explanations, data revisions, model risk, and path dependence. |

A **base case** is the governed reference path, often consensus or committee
approved. An **active view** is a measurable deviation from that path. A stress
case can be useful without being assigned a probability; do not mix
probability-weighted scenarios with deliberately extreme stresses without
labeling the difference.

## Capital market expectation contract

Calling an assumption a capital market expectation (CME) requires more than a
directional asset view:

| Dimension | Minimum declaration |
| --- | --- |
| Expected return | Arithmetic or geometric; nominal or real; horizon and annualization convention; currency |
| Risk | Volatility horizon and annualization, downside measure, or scenario loss |
| Dependence | Correlation or covariance definition, sample, and regime assumption |
| Benchmark | Investable or analytical reference and active deviation |
| Uncertainty | Range, scenario set, or documented reason no interval is available |

The qualitative transmission matrix below is a scenario-design input. It does
not become a CME until those units and horizons are supplied.

## Transmission matrix

| Macro change | Cash-flow channel | Discount-rate channel | Risk-premium channel | Currency channel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stronger growth | Higher sales and employment | Rates may rise if inflation risk increases | Risk appetite may improve | Currency may strengthen if capital inflows rise |
| Higher inflation | Margin pressure and lower real income | Nominal rates and real rates may rise | Inflation uncertainty can widen premia | Currency may weaken if credibility falls |
| Tighter monetary policy | Slower credit and demand | Higher short rates and possible curve changes | Liquidity risk may rise | Currency may strengthen or weaken depending on credibility and growth damage |
| Larger fiscal deficit | Demand support in the short run | Higher term premia possible | Sovereign and crowding-out risk may rise | Currency pressure possible if external funding is weak |
| Global risk-off | Earnings expectations may fall | Safe-haven rates may decline | Credit and equity premia widen | High-beta currencies often weaken |

## Worked example: quantify the transmission bridge

Consider a one-year peso cash flow. The base case assumes expected cash flow of
MXN 105, a relevant rate of 6.0%, and a risk premium of 3.0%:

```{math}
P_{\mathrm{base}}=\frac{105}{1+0.06+0.03}=96.33.
```

A sticky-inflation scenario lowers expected cash flow to MXN 102, raises the
rate to 7.5%, and raises the risk premium to 4.0%:

```{math}
P_{\mathrm{sticky}}=\frac{102}{1+0.075+0.04}=91.48.
```

The modeled value declines by 5.04%. This is a scenario sensitivity, not a
price target: the one-year horizon omits later cash flows, and the assumed rate
and premium shocks may overlap if they are not defined carefully.

With cash flow changed first, let $k=r+\lambda$ denote the combined relevant
rate and risk premium. The sequential attribution is:

```{math}
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathrm{CF}}
&=\frac{102}{1+0.06+0.03}=93.58,\\
\Delta P_{\mathrm{CF}}
&=P_{\mathrm{CF}}-P_{\mathrm{base}}=-2.75,\\
\Delta P_k
&=P_{\mathrm{sticky}}-P_{\mathrm{CF}}=-2.10,\\
\Delta P
&=\Delta P_{\mathrm{CF}}+\Delta P_k=-4.85\ \text{MXN}.
\end{aligned}
```

```{figure} ../../img/generated/m3-scenario-valuation-bridge.png
:alt: A sequential waterfall starts at a base value of 96.33 MXN, subtracts 2.75 MXN for the cash-flow change and 2.10 MXN for the rate-and-risk-premium change, and ends at 91.48 MXN.
:width: 900px
:name: module-3-scenario-valuation-bridge

The bridge makes the cash-flow-first attribution visible. The two contributions
sum exactly to the total change, but a different ordering would allocate the
interaction differently.
```

Sequential attribution depends on order, so an investment memo must state its
attribution convention. The decomposition explains the scenario arithmetic; it
does not identify which macro shock caused either input change.

## Macro surprises

Markets often respond to the difference between released data and expected data, not to the level alone. A useful surprise note records:

1. expected value or consensus;
2. actual release;
3. revision to prior data;
4. immediate market reaction;
5. plausible transmission channel;
6. whether the reaction changes the base case or only the tactical view.

The first dashboard should show levels and changes through time. If a note
attributes a market move to a surprise, it must also preserve the consensus
source and timestamp, the actual release, and any revision. Later versions can
add surprise indices, nowcasting, or release-calendar panels once the source
discipline is stable.

## Nowcast and vintage discipline

A **nowcast** estimates the current but not yet fully observed period; a
**forecast** targets a future period. Nowcasting panels are usually ragged
because series arrive on different release dates. As an editorial contract for
this book, any historical nowcast evaluation must preserve the data vintage and
release calendar that were actually available at each origin. A latest-vintage
panel can illustrate transformations and relationships, but it cannot support a
point-in-time nowcast backtest by itself.

## Dashboard requirements

A macro dashboard should not be a collection of charts. It should answer a defined question.

| Requirement | Minimum standard |
| --- | --- |
| Analytical question | State the investment decision the dashboard supports. |
| Source note | Provider, series id, unit, frequency, date range, and access mode. |
| Revision policy | Explain whether data can be revised and how snapshots are versioned. |
| Visual hierarchy | Separate growth, inflation, rates, FX, fiscal, and market-response panels. |
| Quality flag | Show missingness, stale data, or provider limitations. |
| Interpretation | Summarize what changed, why it matters, and what remains uncertain. |
