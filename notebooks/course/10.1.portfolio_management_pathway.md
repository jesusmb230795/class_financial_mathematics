# Portfolio Management Pathway

Module: Advanced Pathways and Capstone

## Lesson summary

The Portfolio Management pathway asks how an institutional investor should
design, implement, and monitor exposure relative to a mandate. The benchmark is
not background decoration: it defines the opportunity set, active weights,
risk budget, performance attribution, and implementation counterfactual.

This lesson integrates index strategies, active equity, active fixed income,
yield-curve and credit decisions, execution, and institutional governance in
one compact case {cite}`fabozzi2019foundations`.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- compare capitalization-weighted, equal-weighted, fundamental, and
  factor-oriented index designs;
- express active equity and fixed-income positions relative to a benchmark;
- connect curve, duration, key-rate, spread, default, and liquidity views to
  portfolio risk;
- calculate active return and distinguish it from tracking error and the
  information ratio;
- measure implementation shortfall against a declared decision price;
- structure an institutional case with mandate, constraints, governance,
  execution, monitoring, and attribution.

## Prerequisites and notation

Complete the [Module 10 overview](../../chapters/10-capstone.md) and the Module
9 portfolio sequence. This lesson uses:

- \(w_i^P\) and \(w_i^B\): portfolio and benchmark weights;
- \(a_i=w_i^P-w_i^B\): active weight;
- \(R_P\), \(R_B\), and \(R_A=R_P-R_B\): portfolio, benchmark, and active
  return;
- \(\sigma_A\): annualized tracking error under a declared estimator;
- \(IR=\mathbb{E}[R_A]/\sigma_A\): information ratio under compatible horizons.

Weights sum to one unless cash or financing is explicitly modeled. Returns are
simple decimal returns over the stated period.

## Index strategy is a design decision

| Index design | Main rule | Embedded exposure | Implementation issue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Capitalization weighted | weight by investable market value | large issuers and market valuation | concentration and free-float changes |
| Equal weighted | equal constituent weights | smaller-size and rebalancing tilt | higher turnover and capacity limits |
| Fundamental weighted | weight by accounting or economic scale | value and profitability characteristics | metric definitions and reporting lag |
| Factor oriented | target documented factor signals | intended style exposure | model, crowding, turnover, unintended bets |
| Liability or custom | match investor-specific cash-flow or risk objective | duration, inflation, currency, or funding needs | governance and bespoke maintenance |

The index methodology records universe, eligibility, weighting, reconstitution,
rebalancing, corporate actions, currency, withholding-tax convention, pricing
time, and transaction-cost assumptions. Backtested index performance is not
live investable performance.

## Active equity

An active-equity thesis links:

1. differentiated evidence about cash flows, valuation, quality, catalyst, or
   market expectations;
2. an active position sized within factor, sector, liquidity, and loss limits;
3. an implementation route and time horizon;
4. a thesis-break and review trigger.

Active weights alone do not reveal risk. The portfolio also reports beta,
sector, country, currency, style, concentration, liquidity, and scenario
exposures. A stock-specific thesis that merely recreates an unintended factor
bet should be described as such.

## Active fixed income, yield-curve strategies, and credit strategies

Active fixed-income decisions can originate from:

- **duration:** change in parallel-rate sensitivity;
- **curve:** steepening, flattening, butterfly, roll-down, or key-rate view;
- **sector and spread:** government, corporate, securitized, or other spread
  allocation;
- **credit:** issuer selection, migration, default, recovery, seniority, and
  covenant view;
- **inflation and currency:** real-rate, breakeven, and FX exposure;
- **liquidity and financing:** market depth, repo, collateral, and funding.

A curve trade is expressed in key-rate durations or cash-flow sensitivities,
not only in maturity labels. A credit yield is decomposed into reference rate,
credit spread, liquidity premium, optionality, expected loss, and uncertainty.
Yield pick-up is not excess return unless defaults, migration, financing,
hedging, and transaction costs are considered.

## Execution and implementation shortfall

Execution links the paper portfolio to realized holdings. The order record
contains decision time and price, side, quantity, benchmark, urgency, limit,
venue or counterparty, partial fills, fees, taxes, spreads, and market impact.

For a buy order with decision price \(P_d\), execution prices \(P_j\),
quantities \(q_j\), and unexecuted quantity \(q_u\) valued at closing price
\(P_c\), a simplified currency shortfall is:

```{math}
IS=\sum_j q_j(P_j-P_d)+q_u(P_c-P_d)
+\text{Explicit costs}.
```

For sells, the sign convention is reversed so a positive number remains a
cost. The memo states whether shortfall is currency, basis points of order
value, or basis points of portfolio NAV.

## Worked institutional case

A simulated MXN 500m balanced mandate uses this policy benchmark:

| Sleeve | Benchmark weight | Active portfolio weight | Period return |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Equity | 45% | 48% | 8.0% |
| Government bonds | 35% | 32% | 3.0% |
| Credit | 10% | 12% | 5.0% |
| Cash | 10% | 8% | 2.0% |

The benchmark return is:

```{math}
R_B=0.45(0.08)+0.35(0.03)+0.10(0.05)+0.10(0.02)
=5.35\%.
```

The active portfolio return before implementation cost is:

```{math}
R_P=0.48(0.08)+0.32(0.03)+0.12(0.05)+0.08(0.02)
=5.56\%.
```

Gross active return is 0.21%, or 21 basis points. If transition and trading
costs equal 14 basis points of portfolio NAV, net active return is 7 basis
points for the period.

This single observation does not support an information ratio. Estimating
\(IR\) requires a compatible series of active returns and a defensible
tracking-error estimator. Serial correlation and non-normality can invalidate
mechanical scaling {cite}`sharpe1994ratio,lo2002sharpe`.

### Decision interpretation

The 3% equity overweight and 2% credit overweight were funded by government
bonds and cash. The committee should ask:

- Did the active return come from intended sleeve weights or security
  selection within sleeves?
- Did transaction cost consume the forecast edge?
- Does lower cash violate liquidity or collateral limits?
- Are equity and credit overweights one shared growth-risk bet?
- What curve, duration, spread, default, and FX exposures changed?

## Institutional case architecture

| Section | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Mandate | objective, benchmark, horizon, liabilities, permitted instruments |
| Constraints | active risk, concentration, liquidity, leverage, derivatives, currency, sustainability, legal |
| Forecast | capital market assumptions, active theses, confidence, horizon |
| Construction | active weights, factor and key-rate exposures, optimization or judgment |
| Execution | transition plan, capacity, counterparties, cost budget |
| Risk | positive-loss measures, drawdown, stress, liquidity, funding |
| Governance | delegated authority, exceptions, escalation, committee cadence |
| Evaluation | net performance, attribution, implementation shortfall, thesis outcomes |

The case conclusion names what action is approved, the size, the funding
source, the owner, the deadline, and the conditions that cancel the action.

## Checks and limitations

- Do not compare an active portfolio with an unrelated benchmark.
- Preserve index methodology and constituent vintage.
- Separate allocation, selection, currency, and interaction effects.
- Express fixed-income risk with duration, key-rate, spread, credit, and
  liquidity measures, not yield alone.
- Include transaction costs before claiming active value.
- Use positive-loss conventions for VaR and Expected Shortfall.
- A single-period active return is not manager skill.
- The worked case omits within-sleeve security selection, tax, derivatives,
  FX, liabilities, and intra-period cash flows.

## Practice

A simulated benchmark is 50% equity, 30% government bonds, 15% credit, and 5%
cash. A portfolio holds 46%, 34%, 17%, and 3%. Period returns are 6%, 2%, 4%,
and 1%, respectively. Implementation cost is 9 basis points of portfolio NAV.

1. Calculate benchmark and portfolio return.
2. Calculate gross and net active return.
3. Identify the two largest active weights and one shared risk they could
   create.
4. State one execution and one governance control.

```{dropdown} Suggested answer
The benchmark earns \(0.50(0.06)+0.30(0.02)+0.15(0.04)+0.05(0.01)=4.25\%\).
The portfolio earns
\(0.46(0.06)+0.34(0.02)+0.17(0.04)+0.03(0.01)=4.15\%\). Gross active return is
-10 basis points and net active return after 9 basis points of cost is -19
basis points. The largest active weights are +4% government bonds and -4%
equity; both express a lower-growth or lower-risk-rate stance whose duration
and equity-beta consequences should be tested. Controls could include an order
cost limit and committee approval for an active-risk or liquidity exception.
```
