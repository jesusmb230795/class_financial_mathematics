# Accounting Quality and Normalization

Module: Financial Statement Analysis and Financial Modeling

## Lesson summary

Reported earnings combine operating economics with recognition rules,
estimates, financing choices, acquisitions, and unusual events. Normalization
does not mean replacing inconvenient facts with a preferred number. It means
creating a transparent bridge from reported performance to a defined estimate
of sustainable performance.

This lesson reviews the accounting areas that most often alter timing,
classification, comparability, and cash conversion. It then constructs a
normalized EBIT and owner-earnings bridge. The examples are simulations in USD
millions.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- identify recognition and estimation choices that affect revenue, expenses,
  assets, liabilities, and cash flow;
- distinguish non-recurring items from recurring but non-cash expenses;
- analyze inventories, long-lived assets, intangibles, leases, pensions,
  stock-based compensation, and income taxes;
- build a sourced adjustment schedule from reported to normalized EBIT;
- estimate owner earnings without confusing it with free cash flow to the firm
  (FCFF) or free cash flow to equity (FCFE);
- explain how optimistic and conservative accounting can reverse through later
  periods.

## Prerequisites and notation

Complete [Ratios, DuPont, and Capital Efficiency](4.2.ratios_dupont_and_capital_efficiency.md).
Use the module's reported, reclassified, normalized, and forecast layers.

$T$ is a normalized marginal cash tax rate. "Add back" means reverse an
expense that reduced the reported subtotal; "subtract" means reverse a gain or
recognize an omitted recurring cost. Every adjustment must state its tax
treatment.

## What earnings quality means

High-quality earnings are supported by understandable revenue, repeatable
operations, disciplined estimates, and eventual cash realization. Low cash
conversion is not automatically low quality—a growing subscription company can
collect cash before recognizing revenue, while a manufacturer may invest in
working capital before a seasonal sale. The analysis must explain the business
model and timing.

Use four questions:

1. **Recognition:** when is revenue or expense recorded?
2. **Measurement:** which estimates determine the amount?
3. **Classification:** where does the item appear?
4. **Persistence:** is the economic effect expected to recur?

## Recognition and reporting-quality map

### Revenue recognition

Read the revenue policy and contract-balance notes
{cite}`ifrs15Revenue2026`. Determine:

- the promised good or service and the unit of account;
- whether performance occurs at a point in time or over time;
- gross versus net presentation;
- variable consideration, returns, rebates, warranties, and financing effects;
- contract assets, receivables, and deferred or unearned revenue;
- remaining obligations and customer concentration.

Revenue growth can be reported before cash collection. Compare revenue with
receivables, contract assets, deferred revenue, cash receipts, units, prices,
and backlog. Rapid receivable growth or relaxed terms is a prompt for further
work, not proof of manipulation.

### Expense recognition and capitalization

An expenditure recognized immediately reduces current earnings. A capitalized
expenditure creates an asset and moves expense into later depreciation,
amortization, or impairment. The analyst should ask whether the asset produces
probable future benefits and whether capitalization policies are comparable.

Aggressive capitalization can increase current earnings and operating cash
flow classification while creating later expense or impairment. Mechanical
add-backs of depreciation can make this timing choice disappear from EBITDA
without eliminating its economics.

### Non-recurring items

Restructuring, litigation, natural disasters, asset sales, acquisition costs,
impairments, and discontinued operations may warrant separate analysis.
However, a company that records "one-time" restructuring every year has a
recurring economic cost. Use a multi-year frequency table and management's
history of guidance before excluding an item.

## Major accounting areas

| Area | Main judgment | Analytical consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inventory | Cost-flow method, obsolescence, write-downs, production overhead | Gross margin, working capital, asset turnover, and future reversals |
| Long-lived assets | Useful lives, residual values, depreciation method, impairment | EBIT timing, asset base, ROIC, and maintenance capital needs |
| Intangibles | Capitalization, useful life, impairment, acquired versus internally developed assets | Comparability of earnings and invested capital |
| Leases | Lease term, discount rate, variable payments, renewal options | Debt-like obligations, EBITDA, interest, assets, and cash-flow classification |
| Pensions | Discount rate, expected compensation, longevity, plan assets, contributions | Operating cost, financing cost, leverage, and cash funding |
| Stock compensation | Grant-date value, vesting, forfeitures, dilution, tax effects | Expense, non-cash reconciliation, share count, and owner dilution |
| Income taxes | Current versus deferred tax, jurisdiction mix, losses, valuation allowances, uncertain positions | Effective tax rate, cash taxes, NOPAT, and terminal assumptions |

The table is an analytical routing guide, not a substitute for the applicable
recognition and measurement requirements. The principal IFRS references for
these areas include IAS 2, IAS 36, IAS 38, IAS 19, IFRS 2, and IAS 12
{cite}`ias2Inventories2003,ias36Impairment2004,ias38Intangibles2004,ias19EmployeeBenefits2011,ifrs2ShareBasedPayment2004,ias12IncomeTaxes1996`.

### Inventories

Compare inventory growth with sales and COGS. Disaggregate raw materials, work
in process, and finished goods when available. Rising finished goods with weak
sales can signal slower demand; rising raw materials can reflect planned
production or supply-chain protection. Cost-flow choices and write-down rules
must be understood and held consistent before comparing gross margins; an
analytical normalization must not imply that a permitted accounting policy was
an error {cite}`ias2Inventories2003`.

### Long-lived assets and intangibles

Build roll-forwards for gross assets, accumulated depreciation, capital
expenditures, disposals, acquisitions, and currency effects. A long useful life
raises current EBIT but may not change cash economics. Separate maintenance
capital from growth capital using operational evidence rather than assuming all
capital expenditure is maintenance.

Research and development, software, brands, customer relationships, and other
intangibles can be created internally or acquired. Accounting treatment can
make an acquisitive company appear less profitable than an organic peer even
when their economics are similar. Any analytical capitalization or
amortization adjustment needs a consistent life and impairment policy. It also
needs a complete asset and amortization roll-forward so the analyst does not
remove current capitalization while leaving prior-period amortization
untouched {cite}`ias36Impairment2004,ias38Intangibles2004`.

### Leases and pensions

Leases and underfunded pensions can behave like financing claims. Record the
amount, maturity, discount-rate sensitivity, and cash classification before
calculating leverage or enterprise value. Do not add a lease obligation to debt
without also aligning the right-of-use asset, earnings, and cash-flow treatment
{cite}`ifrs16Leases2026,ias19EmployeeBenefits2011`.

### Stock-based compensation

Stock compensation is non-cash in the period, but it transfers value and can
dilute existing owners. Keep it in normalized operating expense by default.
If a valuation adds it back to cash flow, it must separately model the cost of
repurchases required to offset dilution or the future diluted share count
{cite}`ifrs2ShareBasedPayment2004`.

### Income taxes

Reconcile the statutory rate to the reported effective rate, then separate
current cash taxes from deferred tax expense. A temporary low effective rate
from losses, credits, geographic mix, or discrete items should not be
capitalized indefinitely. Match the tax rate to the cash flow and jurisdiction
being valued, and document whether the associated tax benefit is realizable
{cite}`ias12IncomeTaxes1996`.

## Worked example: normalized EBIT

Assume reported EBIT is USD 120m. The notes identify:

- a USD 18m plant-closure charge that is separately disclosed and not expected
  to recur in the forecast period;
- a USD 10m gain on sale of land;
- USD 6m of routine maintenance software work capitalized during the year even
  though the analyst's consistent peer policy treats it as a current operating
  expense;
- USD 12m of stock compensation already included in operating expense.

The adjustment schedule is:

```{math}
\text{Normalized EBIT}
=\text{Reported EBIT}+\sum_i a_i,
```

where $a_i$ is a signed, sourced pretax adjustment. If item $i$ has a
realizable tax rate $T_i$, the item-level after-tax bridge is:

```{math}
\text{Normalized NOPAT}
=\text{Reported EBIT}(1-T_{\mathrm{reported}})
+\sum_i a_i(1-T_i).
```

For this simulation only, every nonzero adjustment and reported EBIT are
deductible or taxable at 25% in the same period:

| Item | EBIT adjustment | Tax assumption | Reason |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Reported EBIT | 120 | 25% | Controlling statement |
| Plant-closure charge | +18 | 25% | Reverse supported non-recurring expense |
| Land-sale gain | (10) | 25% | Remove non-operating gain |
| Routine software cost | (6) | 25% | Apply consistent operating treatment |
| Stock compensation | 0 | Not adjusted | Recurring economic cost; no normalization |
| **Normalized EBIT** | **122** | Item-level bridge | $120+18-10-6$ |

With $T=25\%$:

```{math}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Normalized NOPAT}
&=120(0.75)+18(0.75)-10(0.75)-6(0.75)\\
&=91.5.
\end{aligned}
```

The adjustment does not claim reported EBIT is wrong. It creates a
decision-specific sustainable operating measure. A downside case should test
whether restructuring is actually recurring and whether maintenance software
spending rises.

The software adjustment assumes no related amortization from prior capitalized
spending in this one-period example. A full normalization must add back that
reported amortization, reduce the software asset, and reclassify current
spending between CFO and CFI without changing total cash:

```{math}
\text{Normalized EBIT}
=\text{Reported EBIT}
-\text{Current capitalized operating spend}
+\text{Related reported amortization}.
```

The bridge changes EBIT by a net USD 2m. Stock compensation remains in

## Owner earnings

Owner earnings is an informal estimate of cash that could be distributed while
maintaining the business's competitive and operating capacity. A simplified
form is:

```{math}
\text{Owner earnings}
\approx
\text{Normalized net income}
+\text{D\&A and eligible non-cash charges}
-\text{Maintenance capital expenditures}
-\Delta\text{Required operating working capital}.
```

If normalized net income is USD 78m, D&A is USD 30m, estimated maintenance
capital expenditure is USD 24m, and required operating working capital rises by
USD 12m:

```{math}
\text{Owner earnings}=78+30-24-12=72.
```

Eligible non-cash add-backs exclude recurring stock compensation unless the
analysis separately models offsetting repurchases or dilution. Owner earnings
is not automatically FCFF because it begins after interest, and it is not
automatically FCFE because it omits net borrowing and may define capital needs
differently. Label the measure and reconcile it to the cash-flow definition
used in valuation {cite}`damodaran2012investment`.

## Review controls

Maintain an adjustment register:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Filing, note, page, table, period, and vintage |
| Reported subtotal | Exact starting amount and sign |
| Adjustment | Pretax amount and direction |
| Tax | Current, deferred, or no tax effect, with rate |
| Classification | Operating, financing, investing, or non-operating |
| Persistence | Recurring, transitional, event-specific, or uncertain |
| Cash effect | Past, current, future, or non-cash |
| Scenario treatment | Base, downside, upside, or excluded |

An adjustment without this information is an opinion, not an auditable model
input.

## Checks and limitations

- Normalization can become biased when every unfavorable item is excluded and
  every favorable item is retained.
- Reported non-cash expenses can still represent real economic costs.
- Management-defined adjusted metrics may change definitions between periods.
- Maintenance capital expenditure is rarely reported directly and must be
  estimated with operating evidence.
- Deferred taxes can reverse far beyond the explicit forecast horizon.
- Legal and accounting rules differ by jurisdiction; this lesson is an
  analytical framework, not accounting or audit advice.

## Practice

Reported EBIT is USD 200m and includes a USD 25m plant-closure charge, a USD
15m disposal gain, and USD 8m of recurring stock compensation. The closure is
supported as non-recurring for the forecast horizon. For the separate
owner-earnings bridge, normalized net income is USD 120m, D&A is USD 35m,
estimated maintenance capital expenditure is USD 40m, and required operating
working capital increases by USD 10m. Use $T=25\%$.

1. Calculate normalized EBIT and NOPAT.
2. Explain the treatment of stock compensation.
3. Estimate owner earnings from the provided normalized net-income starting
   point.
4. Name two facts that could reverse the normalization conclusion.

```{dropdown} Suggested answer
Normalized EBIT is $200+25-15=210$ USD m. Stock compensation remains in
operating expense because it is recurring and dilutive, even though it is
non-cash in the current period. Normalized NOPAT is $210(1-0.25)=157.5$ USD
m. Owner earnings from the normalized net-income starting point are
$120+35-40-10=105$ USD m. The conclusion could change if plant closures recur
as part of the operating model, if the disposal is part of a recurring asset
rotation strategy, if the tax effects differ, or if maintenance capital
expenditure is understated.
```
