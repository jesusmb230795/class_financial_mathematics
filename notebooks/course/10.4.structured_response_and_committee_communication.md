# Structured Response and Committee Communication

Module: Advanced Pathways and Capstone

## Lesson summary

Investment work is useful only when a reviewer can see the decision, evidence,
calculation, uncertainty, and requested action. This lesson introduces the
`DECIDE` response protocol, a one-page memo architecture, three pathway labs,
and an investment-committee presentation standard. The format is intentionally
compact so it can expose weak evidence instead of hiding it behind a long
report.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- frame an investment question as a specific decision;
- separate observed evidence, transformations, and assumptions;
- present calculations with currency, unit, date, and convention;
- write a recommendation with alternatives, conditions, and thesis-breaks;
- distinguish a technical memo from an executive decision memo;
- answer committee questions without overstating model certainty;
- design a presentation whose charts and tables support one decision.

## Prerequisites and notation

Read the three pathway lessons and complete at least one worked case. This
lesson uses basis points (`bp`), where 100 bp = 1 percentage point. Loss
measures remain non-negative magnitudes. Every value carries an as-of date,
currency, unit, and observed or modeled status.

## The DECIDE protocol

| Step | Required content | Failure signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D — Define** | decision, decision maker, horizon, scope, and deadline | open-ended topic instead of a decision |
| **E — Establish evidence** | controlling sources, vintage, units, quality, and rights | unsupported number or stale source |
| **C — Calculate** | formulas, assumptions, scenarios, sensitivities, and checks | point estimate without reconciliation |
| **I — Interpret** | mechanism, economic meaning, benchmark, and trade-off | description without decision relevance |
| **D — Disclose** | limitations, conflicts, uncertainty, and thesis-breaks | caveat list disconnected from action |
| **E — Execute and evaluate** | action, owner, size, funding, trigger, monitoring, and attribution | recommendation without implementation |

A short answer can satisfy DECIDE. A long answer can fail it.

## Evidence table before prose

Begin the response with the material inputs:

| Claim or input | Value | Status | Source and date | Transformation | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example | MXN 100m | observed | filing, 2026-06-30 | none | reporting lag |
| Example | 8.0% | assumption | committee base case | scenario | not market quote |

This table prevents a forecast from acquiring the visual authority of an
observed value. It also gives reviewers a direct list of items to challenge.

## One-page investment memo

### Decision header

- **Decision:** one sentence stating the action under consideration.
- **Recommendation:** approve, reject, defer, or approve conditionally.
- **Amount and funding:** currency, size, source, and implementation date.
- **Owner:** accountable person or committee.
- **As of:** evidence and market date.

### Core body

1. **Why now:** event, opportunity, obligation, or scheduled review.
2. **Evidence:** three to five controlling facts with sources.
3. **Economics:** valuation, return, cost, and cash-flow bridge.
4. **Risk:** positive-loss measure, named stresses, liquidity, concentration,
   and non-quantifiable risks.
5. **Alternatives:** including no action and delay.
6. **Conditions:** evidence or controls required before execution.
7. **Monitoring:** threshold, frequency, owner, and pre-agreed response.

### Appendices

Keep detailed models, data dictionaries, methodology, legal evidence indexes,
and secondary charts outside the decision page. The memo must remain
reconcilable to them.

## Technical memo versus executive memo

| Technical memo | Executive memo |
| --- | --- |
| proves method, calculation, checks, and sensitivity | identifies decision, reason, trade-off, and action |
| includes equations, data lineage, parameter choices | includes only material outputs and triggers |
| written for analytical challenge and reproduction | written for governance and allocation of authority |
| can be several pages | normally one or two pages |

Compression occurs after the analysis is complete. Removing units,
limitations, or conditions is not executive communication.

## Worked response

**Prompt:** A MXN 500m institutional portfolio earned 21 bp gross active return
and paid 14 bp of implementation cost. Should the committee increase its
active-risk budget immediately?

**Structured answer:**

- **Define:** Decide whether to increase the annual active-risk limit today.
- **Evidence:** One period produced 7 bp net active return; the observation has
  not been attributed by sleeve or security.
- **Calculate:** \(21-14=7\) bp net. A one-period information ratio is not
  defined.
- **Interpret:** The result is positive but too short and too aggregated to
  establish repeatable skill. Cost consumed two-thirds of gross active return.
- **Disclose:** Benchmark fit, factor exposures, transaction timing, and
  attribution are unresolved.
- **Execute and evaluate:** Defer the risk-limit increase. Require a
  multi-period net attribution, capacity review, and thesis-level hit-rate
  analysis; reconsider at the next scheduled committee review.

The answer reaches a decision without inventing statistical confidence.

## Structured response labs

### Lab A: Portfolio Management

An active-fixed-income proposal adds duration and credit spread exposure while
reducing cash. Write a 250-word DECIDE response that:

1. names the benchmark and active exposures;
2. reports expected return after implementation cost;
3. includes one parallel-rate, one curve, one spread, and one liquidity stress;
4. states an approval limit and monitoring trigger.

### Lab B: Private Markets

A buyout case shows a \(2.5\times\) gross MOIC, but 25% of exit equity value
depends on multiple expansion and the fund waterfall is not final. Write a
conditional recommendation that:

1. separates operating, deleveraging, and multiple effects;
2. identifies agreement evidence still required;
3. translates gross deal proceeds into a provisional net-investor range;
4. states what would cause rejection.

### Lab C: Private Wealth

A family has 70% of reported wealth and most annual income in one private
company, while stressed three-year liquid resources cover only 0.81 times
required outflows. Write a response that:

1. prioritizes the immediate liquidity decision;
2. presents at least three concentration alternatives;
3. preserves control, tax, legal, and family-governance questions;
4. names the specialists and approvals required before execution.

```{dropdown} Lab review standard
Each response receives credit only when it makes a decision, distinguishes
observed evidence from assumptions, shows at least one calculation with units,
names an alternative, states a material limitation, and assigns an owner and
trigger. A recommendation that merely repeats the highest modeled return does
not pass.
```

## Investment-committee presentation

A concise committee deck normally contains:

1. decision and recommendation;
2. mandate or client fit;
3. controlling evidence and data quality;
4. economics and valuation range;
5. risk, liquidity, and named stress scenarios;
6. alternatives and trade-offs;
7. implementation, conditions, and owners;
8. monitoring dashboard and thesis-breaks.

Charts use descriptive titles that state the analytical point, direct labels,
currency and units, sample dates, source notes, and observed-versus-modeled
status. The visual hierarchy should help a reader find exceptions and actions,
not decorate the result {cite}`few2006dashboard,knaflic2015storytelling`.

## Committee question discipline

When challenged:

- answer the decision-relevant question first;
- state whether the answer is observed, calculated, assumed, or unknown;
- show the source or calculation;
- quantify sensitivity when possible;
- do not change a model assumption silently during the meeting;
- log unresolved questions, owner, evidence needed, and decision impact.

"I do not know; this evidence is required before approval" is stronger than an
unsupported estimate.

## Checks and limitations

- Do not bury the requested action at the end.
- Never remove currency, unit, date, horizon, or sign to save space.
- A dashboard and deck must reconcile to the technical model.
- Distinguish a thesis-break from routine volatility.
- Include no-action and delay when they are feasible alternatives.
- Record conflicts and dissent.
- Communication quality cannot repair missing evidence.

## Practice

Choose one pathway lab and produce:

1. a six-row evidence table;
2. a 200- to 300-word DECIDE response;
3. a three-sentence executive summary;
4. two committee questions that could reverse the recommendation.

```{dropdown} Suggested structure
The evidence table should contain at least two observed inputs, two
transformations or calculations, and two assumptions. The DECIDE response
should conclude with action, owner, date, condition, and trigger. The executive
summary should state recommendation, central reason, and largest unresolved
risk. The reversal questions should target the thesis or implementation, not
minor formatting.
```
