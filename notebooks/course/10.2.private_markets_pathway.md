# Private Markets Pathway

Module: Advanced Pathways and Capstone

## Lesson summary

The Private Markets pathway asks whether capital should be committed to a deal
or one of several possible fund vehicles, on what terms, and through which
realization plan. Valuation is
necessary but insufficient. The decision also depends on ownership rights,
financing, covenants, governance, value-creation capacity, follow-on funding,
fees, capital calls, distributions, and exit control.

This lesson turns a deal screen into an investment-committee case and connects
company-level proceeds to fund- and investor-level economics.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, readers should be able to:

- build a screening funnel with explicit rejection and escalation criteria;
- connect operating diligence to a valuation and financing structure;
- distinguish company, security, vehicle, and investor cash flows;
- identify the controlling agreements and rights needed to support a modeled
  claim;
- calculate entry equity, exit equity, MOIC, and IRR under stated assumptions;
- design a value-creation plan with owners, milestones, capital, and downside
  controls;
- evaluate exits, capital calls, distributions, management fees, and carried
  interest in the investment recommendation.

## Prerequisites and notation

Complete [Private Capital Structures and Fund Economics](8.1.private_capital_structures_and_economics.md),
[Private-Company Valuation and Due Diligence](8.2.private_company_valuation_and_due_diligence.md),
and the [Module 10 overview](../../chapters/10-capstone.md).

The worked case uses:

- \(EBITDA_0\), \(EBITDA_T\): normalized entry and exit EBITDA;
- \(M_0\), \(M_T\): entry and exit EV/EBITDA multiples;
- \(D_0\), \(D_T\): debt at entry and exit;
- \(E_0\), \(E_T\): sponsor equity invested and exit equity proceeds;
- \(T\): holding period in years.

Amounts are MXN millions. Multiples are ratios, and IRR uses annual periods in
the simplified example.

## Deal screening

A screening funnel prevents deep diligence from becoming an argument for a
deal that never fit the mandate:

| Gate | Minimum question | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mandate | Is the asset, geography, stage, check size, and risk permitted? | fund documents and allocation limits |
| Thesis | What market or operating inefficiency creates value? | customer, industry, and company evidence |
| Claim | What security and rights will the investor own? | term sheet and cap table |
| Economics | Is value supported under a conservative case? | valuation, financing, dilution, and exit range |
| Execution | Can the team source, close, govern, and improve the asset? | workplan, references, operating resources |
| Downside | What causes permanent loss and what protects capital? | stress, covenants, collateral, liquidity |
| Conflicts | Who benefits from fees, allocation, related parties, or exit? | agreements and conflict register |

Each gate has `pass`, `fail`, or `escalate` status, an owner, and an evidence
date. "Interesting opportunity" is not a gate result.

## Valuation and structure

Company value is triangulated using discounted cash flow, comparable
companies, precedent transactions, and scenario evidence
{cite}`damodaran2012investment`. Deal structure then determines the investor's
claim:

- common, preferred, convertible, debt, or hybrid security;
- price, ownership, liquidation preference, participation, anti-dilution;
- coupon, payment-in-kind terms, maturity, amortization, security, covenants;
- board, information, veto, reserved-matter, and exit rights;
- management rollover, incentive pool, earnout, and contingent consideration;
- FX, tax, legal, regulatory, and closing conditions.

The valuation memo and cap table must reproduce the proceeds waterfall under
each relevant exit value. A proportional equity-share calculation is
insufficient when security rights differ.

## Agreements and fund vehicle

The evidence index identifies the purpose and current version of:

| Document | Decision supported |
| --- | --- |
| Term sheet | principal economic and control terms |
| Purchase or subscription agreement | consideration, representations, closing, indemnity |
| Shareholders agreement | governance, transfer, pre-emption, drag/tag, exit |
| Debt documents | funding, collateral, covenants, default, remedies |
| Management incentive plan | dilution, vesting, performance, leaver terms |
| Fund or trust agreement | mandate, fees, waterfall, term, conflicts |
| Side letters | investor-specific rights and obligations |
| Disclosure schedules | exceptions to representations and known exposures |

This page is an analytical checklist, not legal advice. Qualified legal and tax
review is part of the decision when the terms require it. Fund agreements,
governance, conflicts, fees, and investor reporting should be evaluated against
the executed documents; institutional principles support that review without
overriding the contract {cite}`ilpaPrinciples2019`.

## Value-creation plan

A value-creation claim becomes testable when it has:

| Driver | Baseline | Target and date | Owner | Capital required | Failure trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Revenue | customer/price/volume baseline | measurable operating milestone | operating lead | amount | loss of customer or missed cohort |
| Margin | normalized cost base | procurement, mix, automation, or utilization | management | amount | service or quality deterioration |
| Working capital | days and seasonal profile | cash-conversion target | finance lead | amount | covenant or supplier stress |
| Capital structure | debt, maturity, rate, covenants | deleveraging/refinancing target | CFO/sponsor | amount | coverage or refinancing breach |
| Governance | reporting and decision gaps | board/control milestone | sponsor | amount | unresolved conflict or audit issue |

The plan distinguishes operational improvement from multiple expansion. Exit
multiple is a market assumption, not value creation.

## Worked buyout case

A simulated company has normalized entry EBITDA of MXN 12m. The sponsor buys at
\(8.0\times\) EV/EBITDA with MXN 48m of acquisition debt:

```{math}
EV_0=12\times8=\text{MXN }96\text{m},
```

```{math}
E_0=96-48=\text{MXN }48\text{m}.
```

After five years, the base case assumes EBITDA of MXN 18m, the same
\(8.0\times\) exit multiple, and debt reduced to MXN 24m:

```{math}
EV_T=18\times8=\text{MXN }144\text{m},
```

```{math}
E_T=144-24=\text{MXN }120\text{m}.
```

Before fees, carry, taxes, interim cash flows, and dilution:

```{math}
MOIC=\frac{120}{48}=2.50\times,
```

```{math}
IRR=(2.50)^{1/5}-1=20.11\%.
```

### Exit-multiple sensitivity

| Exit multiple | Exit EV | Exit equity | Gross MOIC | Gross five-year IRR |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(7.0\times\) | MXN 126m | MXN 102m | \(2.125\times\) | 16.27% |
| \(8.0\times\) | MXN 144m | MXN 120m | \(2.500\times\) | 20.11% |
| \(9.0\times\) | MXN 162m | MXN 138m | \(2.875\times\) | 23.52% |

The memo also separates value sources. From entry to base exit:

- EBITDA growth adds operating value under a constant-multiple convention;
- debt reduction transfers enterprise value to equity but requires cash;
- exit-multiple change contributes zero in the base case;
- fees, taxes, incentive dilution, and fund carry reduce investor proceeds.

Because these contributions interact, the attribution order must be stated.

## Exit and realization plan

Potential exits include strategic sale, sponsor-to-sponsor sale, public
offering, recapitalization, refinancing, or orderly wind-down. The plan records:

- earliest feasible date and preparation milestones;
- buyer or capital-provider universe;
- financial, operational, governance, and audit readiness;
- debt repayment, transaction cost, tax, preference, and dilution waterfall;
- market, financing, regulatory, and execution risks;
- alternatives if the preferred exit is unavailable.

An exit is not an assumption that "the market will pay the modeled multiple."

## Capital calls, distributions, and carry

The company model hands dated equity cash flows to the fund model. The fund
model then includes:

- deal contributions and follow-on reserves;
- management fees and fund expenses;
- fund financing and its repayment;
- realizations and other distributions;
- recycling and unfunded commitment;
- preferred return, catch-up, carried interest, escrow, and clawback;
- investor-specific side-letter effects when applicable.

Gross deal IRR and net LP IRR are different outputs. The investment committee
sees both, along with DPI, RVPI, TVPI, and the valuation status of unrealized
assets.

## Checks and limitations

- Reject deals outside the mandate before detailed modeling.
- Reconcile the security claim and cap table to every exit scenario.
- Do not count multiple expansion as operational value creation.
- Stress refinancing, working capital, covenants, dilution, and follow-on
  funding.
- Separate gross deal, gross fund, and net investor economics.
- Preserve agreement versions and unresolved diligence.
- The worked example assumes one contribution, one exit, no interim cash flow,
  and no fund-level fee or carry.

## Practice

A simulated transaction has entry EBITDA of MXN 20m, entry multiple
\(7.5\times\), and MXN 60m debt. After four years, EBITDA is MXN 25m, debt is
MXN 35m, and the exit multiple is \(7.0\times\).

1. Calculate entry equity, exit equity, gross MOIC, and gross annualized IRR.
2. State whether multiple change created or destroyed value.
3. Name one agreement right and one operating milestone needed before approval.

```{dropdown} Suggested answer
Entry EV is \(20\times7.5=\) MXN 150m and entry equity is MXN 90m. Exit EV is
\(25\times7=\) MXN 175m and exit equity is MXN 140m. Gross MOIC is
\(140/90=1.556\times\), and four-year IRR is
\((140/90)^{1/4}-1=11.68\%\). Multiple contraction destroyed value relative to
a constant-multiple case even though EBITDA growth and debt reduction increased
equity. A board or information right and a measurable margin, customer,
working-capital, or deleveraging milestone would be appropriate if tied to the
specific thesis.
```
